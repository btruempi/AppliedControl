    Learning Graph — Valve Automation Knowledge Map

    The Learning Graph is the brain of this textbook. It maps all 490 concepts in the Applied Control curriculum as a directed acyclic graph (DAG) — each node is a concept, each edge represents a prerequisite dependency. Before you can truly understand a concept, you need its prerequisites.

    This structure, pioneered by Dan McCreary's ibook-skills methodology, gives you something a traditional table of contents cannot: a knowledge topology that shows what to learn first, what unlocks what, and which concepts are the most foundational.

    How to Use This Graph

    • Node size reflects the Concept Impact Score (CIS) — how many downstream concepts depend on this one. Large nodes are the most foundational.
    • Node color reflects the taxonomy category (see legend).
    • Click a node to see its definition, prerequisites, and what it unlocks.
    • Use the search box to find any concept by name.
    • Filter by category using the legend buttons.
    • Drag and zoom to explore the full graph.

    CIS — Concept Impact Score

    The Concept Impact Score is computed using a PageRank-style recursion across the dependency graph:

    CIS(x) = 1 + Σ CIS(d) for all direct dependents of x

    A high CIS means that understanding this concept unlocks a large portion of the curriculum. The top 10 are the concepts every Applied Control sales engineer must master first.

    Rank Concept CIS Why it matters
    1 Fluid Mechanics 7,143 Foundation of all valve and flow behavior
    2 Pressure 5,031 Every valve, sensor, and system depends on pressure
    3 Bernoulli Equation 2,170 Drives sizing, cavitation analysis, pressure drop
    4 Continuity Equation 2,103 Governs flow area and velocity relationships
    5 Pressure Drop 2,100 The design parameter every valve controls
    6 Control Valve 1,312 The product category that unifies the portfolio
    7 States of Matter 676 Determines liquid vs. gas vs. two-phase sizing
    8 Viscosity 423 Affects Reynolds number, friction, sizing corrections
    9 Reynolds Number 398 Determines flow regime, noise, and discharge coefficient
    10 Flow Regimes 397 Laminar vs. turbulent governs all flow behavior
