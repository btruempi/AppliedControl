    What No Catalog Can Provide

    1. Correct First-Time Specification

    A control valve specified incorrectly costs 5× its purchase price in rework, re-engineering, and delays. Applied Control's applications engineers run the full sizing calculation — Cv, noise, cavitation index, actuator force balance, fail-safe spring — before the order is placed.

    Dollar Impact — Specification Error Cost

    Industry studies show that 30–40% of control valve problems are caused by misspecification at the time of order. A $15,000 Fisher ED valve that's the wrong size or trim costs:

    • $15,000 valve (wrong spec, returned)
    • $15,000 replacement valve (correct spec, expedited)
    • $8,000 engineering labor to diagnose and re-specify
    • $40,000–$200,000 production loss during delay

    Applied Control's engineering review eliminates this risk.

    2. DVC6200 Diagnostic Programs — The Predictive Maintenance Differentiator

    Applied Control is one of the few distributors in the region that runs complete HART-based predictive maintenance programs using AMS Device Manager. This is not a product sale — it's an ongoing service relationship that prevents shutdowns.

    How the DVC6200 Program Works

    1. Baseline — Applied Control installs DVC6200 positioners on critical valves and records baseline friction signature, travel, and supply pressure
    2. Monitor — AMS Device Manager continuously aggregates HART data from all DVC6200s and alerts when friction increases, travel deviates, or supply pressure drops
    3. Alert — Applied Control's team reviews alerts and contacts the customer: "Valve FV-2301 friction increased 40% from baseline — schedule a packing inspection at your next opportunity"
    4. Prevent — The maintenance team adjusts packing or plans a replacement — before the valve fails in service

    The result: Emerson data shows active DVC6200 monitoring programs reduce valve-caused unplanned shutdowns by 60–80%.

    3. PRV Testing — OSHA PSM Compliance Made Easy

    OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119 (Process Safety Management) requires documented safety relief valve testing as part of Mechanical Integrity. Applied Control's National Board certified shop:

    • Tests all PRVs to ±3% of set pressure per API 576
    • Provides a complete test data package for each valve — the documentation you show OSHA
    • Recertifies to ASME Code
    • Offers same-day turnaround on standard PRVs
    • Inspects for seat corrosion, spring set, and internal condition — findings documented

    Dollar Impact — PRV Testing Cost Comparison

    Approach Cost Per PRV Hidden Costs
    Remove in-plant, ship to shop $800–$1,200 parts + labor Freight, scaffolding, lost production during downtime
    In-house testing (without National Board cert) N/A OSHA citation risk — documentation may not satisfy PSM audit
    Applied Control National Board certified $1,200–$2,000 all-in None — complete data package, OSHA-ready

    4. Pharmaceutical and Life Sciences — IQ/OQ Documentation

    FDA 21 CFR Part 11 and Part 210/211 require Installation Qualification and Operational Qualification documentation for every instrument and valve in a regulated process. Applied Control provides pre-written IQ/OQ templates for Fisher sanitary valves, Rosemount transmitters, and DeltaV systems — saving 8–15 hours of engineering per instrument per installation.

    For a 200-instrument pharmaceutical project, that's 1,600–3,000 hours of engineering documentation saved.
