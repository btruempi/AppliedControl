    Applied Control Sales Toolkit

    Your Field Reference — From First Call to Close

    Objection handling, competitive positioning, product selection matrices, ROI quick-math, and closing scripts — everything a sales engineer needs organized by situation, not by product.

    How to Use This Toolkit

    This page is organized by sales situation, not by product. Find the conversation you're in, find the script or reference that fits, and go. The chapter links give you the technical depth to back up every claim.

    Part 1 — The Discovery Conversation

    The 7 Questions That Unlock Every Account

    Before you can sell anything, you need to understand what keeps the plant manager's phone ringing at 2 AM. These seven questions open every door.

    Field Story — The Opening Conversation

    You: "Before I show you any products, can I ask — in the last year, what has cost you the most unexpected downtime? Not planned maintenance — something that surprised you."

    What you learn: Whether the account is valve-driven, instrumentation-driven, or mechanical-equipment-driven. This tells you which ROI calculator to use and which chapter of Applied Control's capability to open first.

    Question 1: "What's your most unreliable control loop?"

    • The valve that hunts, sticks, or fails most often
    • Answer maps to: DVC6200 diagnostics, friction trending, valve replacement
    • Chapter reference: Chapter 14 — Asset Reliability

    Question 2: "How do you currently test your safety relief valves?"

    • Are they removing PRVs from service for testing? How often?
    • Answer maps to: Applied Control PRV testing program, National Board certification
    • Chapter reference: Chapter 7 — Pressure Relief Systems

    Question 3: "Do you have any critical measurement points you're not monitoring because wiring is too expensive?"

    • Remote tanks, rotating equipment, heat exchangers
    • Answer maps to: Rosemount WirelessHART — 648 temperature, 3051 wireless pressure
    • Chapter reference: Chapter 13 — Measurement

    Question 4: "Where are you losing measurement accuracy that costs you money?"

    • Custody transfer, billing points, critical flow measurement
    • Answer maps to: Micro Motion ELITE Coriolis, Rosemount 3051S SuperModule
    • Chapter reference: Chapter 12 — Flow Measurement

    Question 5: "Do you have any pharmaceutical validation or FDA documentation requirements?"

    • IQ/OQ documentation, 21 CFR Part 11, WFI loops
    • Answer maps to: Fisher sanitary valves, Rosemount 644S, Applied Control IQ/OQ package
    • Chapter reference: Chapter 17 — Life Sciences

    Question 6: "What does an unplanned shutdown cost you per hour?"

    • The number that anchors every ROI calculation
    • Answer maps to: DVC6200 program ROI, DeltaV SIS alarm rationalization
    • Tool: ROI Calculator Suite →

    Question 7: "Who specifies your control valves and instruments — purchasing, operations, or engineering?"

    • Determines your primary contact and the right conversation
    • Engineering: Lead with technical specifications and HART diagnostics
    • Operations: Lead with reliability and the cost of the last failure
    • Purchasing: Lead with lifecycle cost and the Applied Control PRV program

    Part 2 — Competitive Positioning

    Fisher Control Valves vs. Competitor Valves

    Comparison Point Fisher (Applied Control) Competitor (Flowserve, Emerson competitors) Your Script
    Positioner integration DVC6200 is native — factory-mounted, factory-calibrated, signature captured Bolt-on positioner from a third party — separate calibration, no factory baseline "The DVC6200 captures the valve's as-new signature at the factory. Every future diagnostic compares to that baseline — you know if the valve is degrading."
    Diagnostic data Friction, travel, supply pressure, output pressure — all transmitted via HART 7 continuously Most competitors: go/no-go position feedback only "We tell you not just whether the valve moved, but how hard it had to work to get there."
    SIL certification DVC6200 TÜV SIL 2 — independent body, documented proof test Competitor positioners: may have SIL claims but often manufacturer-self-certified "TÜV is who your insurer and regulator recognize. A manufacturer's own SIL claim doesn't satisfy an IEC 61511 audit."
    Repair ecosystem Applied Control National Board certified shop — teardown, repair, recertification Most distributors: ship back to factory or to a third-party shop with no Applied Control oversight "We repair the valve in our shop, run diagnostics before and after, and give you a DVC6200 signature that proves the valve is as good as new."
    Trim selection depth Cavitrol III anti-cavitation, WhisperFlo noise, anti-erosion, cryogenic — engineered for the application General-purpose trim from most competitors — customer specifies wrong trim more often "Our engineers size the trim. We run the Cv calculations, the cavitation index, and the noise prediction before we quote. Wrong trim costs $50K in damage. The sizing conversation is free."
    Parts availability Applied Control stocks Fisher parts — not drop-ship lead time Most distributors: 3–8 week lead time for anything non-standard "We stock it. When your valve goes down at 2 AM, that matters."

    Rosemount vs. Competitor Transmitters

    Comparison Point Rosemount (Applied Control) Honeywell, Yokogawa, ABB Your Script
    10-year stability ±0.1%/10 years — calibration interval extensible to 5 years Most competitors: ±0.2%/5 years — more frequent calibration required "At 500 transmitters, extending calibration from 2 years to 5 years eliminates 750 calibration events over 10 years. At $200 each, that's $150K in avoided maintenance."
    HART 7 diagnostics Full device diagnostics, AMS Device Manager native integration Some competitors still on HART 5 — fewer diagnostic parameters "HART 7 gives us device health, process alerts, and calibration records in AMS without any additional work. HART 5 devices require separate polling infrastructure."
    WirelessHART Rosemount 702/703/704 adapters — any HART device becomes wireless Limited wireless adapter options from most competitors "We can wireless-enable the existing Honeywell transmitters your customer already has — they don't have to rip and replace. But for new instruments, the Rosemount wireless family is self-forming and self-healing."
    SIL rating 3051S: SIL 2 standalone, SIL 3 with redundancy — TÜV certified Many competitors: SIL 1 standalone, SIL 2 with redundancy "If your SIL calculation requires SIL 2 on a single transmitter, most competitor devices require redundancy. Rosemount 3051S does SIL 2 single — simpler architecture, lower installed cost."
    PlantWeb / AMS integration Native — no driver development needed Competitor devices require EDD/FDT development — often missing parameters "When you open a Rosemount device in AMS, every parameter is there out of the box. Competitor devices often have incomplete device descriptions — you get the measurement but not the diagnostic data."

    Micro Motion Coriolis vs. Competitor Flow Technologies

    Technology Accuracy Applied Control Recommendation When to Use It
    Micro Motion ELITE Coriolis ±0.05% mass Lead recommendation for custody transfer and chemical When billing accuracy, density measurement, or mass flow precision matters
    Rosemount 8800D Vortex ±0.65% For steam and clean liquid where mass ±0.65% acceptable Steam metering, general utilities, where no moving parts is the primary need
    Rosemount 8750W Mag ±0.5% For conductive liquids, slurries, pulp stock When fluid would plug or erode other technologies
    Rosemount 3051 DP Orifice ±0.5–2% Lowest cost, widest application When accuracy < ±0.5% acceptable and existing taps in place
    Competitor Coriolis (Endress+Hauser, ABB) ±0.1% — When customer insists on alternate brand — Applied Control can support but preference is Micro Motion

    Coriolis ROI Script: "What is your product worth per unit volume? If you're measuring 1 million gallons per year at $5/gallon, you have $5M on the line. A ±0.5% DP meter accepts $25,000 in measurement error every year. Micro Motion at ±0.05% brings that to $2,500. For a $15,000 meter, you recover the cost in 7 months and then keep $22,500/year in billing accuracy. What's your biggest custody transfer flow?"

    Part 4 — Handling Common Objections

    "We already have [Competitor Brand] installed everywhere."

    Don't argue — agree, then pivot:

    "Absolutely — and we're not here to rip and replace what's working. Here's the question: which of your [Competitor Brand] instruments are on critical loops where you can't afford drift or failure? Those are the points where Rosemount's 10-year stability or the DVC6200's continuous friction monitoring pays for a targeted upgrade. Can you show me your last two unplanned shutdowns and what caused them?"

    "Fisher valves cost more than our current supplier."

    The total cost answer:

    "The valve body is maybe 30% of the total cost of valve ownership over 10 years. The other 70% is maintenance labor, emergency repair, and production loss from failures. Let's run the numbers. What does an unplanned trip cost you? How many trips per year are valve-related? With the DVC6200 diagnostics, most customers see a 60–70% reduction in valve-related shutdowns. That math usually shows a Fisher valve paying for itself in the first year."

    Follow up by opening the DVC6200 ROI Calculator →

    "Our purchasing department only buys on price."

    Engage the operations team:

    "Understood. Can I ask — does purchasing make the decision about which PRVs to use, or does your safety team? And who owns the budget when there's an unplanned trip? In most plants, purchasing sees the valve cost, but operations owns the downtime cost. Sometimes those are different conversations with different people. Who should I be talking to about the reliability side?"

    "We have an internal valve shop — we don't need Applied Control for repair."

    The National Board certification answer:

    "For general valve repair, that makes sense. Here's the question: who provides the official test documentation for your PRVs? OSHA PSM Mechanical Integrity [29 CFR 1910.119] requires that safety relief valves be tested by a National Board certified entity. Your internal shop would need its own National Board certification to satisfy that requirement — or you use Applied Control's shop. What's your current PRV test documentation process?"

    "WirelessHART isn't reliable enough for our process."

    The reliability data answer:

    "Let's separate control from monitoring. WirelessHART is not designed for closed-loop control — and we're not proposing it there. It's designed for monitoring: steam trap temperatures, remote tank levels, heat exchanger temperatures, rotating equipment. For those applications, an update rate of 15–60 seconds is perfectly adequate, and the self-healing mesh network provides >99% data availability. Where are the new monitoring points you need that don't have wiring budget?"

    "We're happy with our current DCS — we don't need DeltaV."

    The AMS integration angle (not a DCS replacement pitch):

    "We're not here to replace your DCS. Here's a different conversation: do your HART-capable devices actually send their diagnostic data to your current DCS? Most plants have HART devices that are wired to non-HART-capable I/O — the diagnostic data never makes it to the control room. AMS Device Manager can sit alongside any DCS and aggregate HART data from all your Fisher and Rosemount devices. No DCS replacement needed."

    Every catalog distributor can order Fisher valves. Applied Control does things no catalog distributor can:

    Fisher valve repair and rework National Board certified shop, on-site Ship to factory — 6–12 week lead time
    PRV testing and recertification Our bench, our NB cert, our documentation Not available

    Application engineering Cv sizing, cavitation index, noise prediction, SIL assessment Not available
    Field service Applied Control field technicians on-site Not available

    AMS Device Manager deployment Configure, commission, and train on-site Not available

    The Emerson Impact Partner Difference

    Emerson has three tiers of partnership. Impact Partner is the top tier — it requires: - Factory training and certification for all product lines - Minimum volume commitments - Technical support capability - Demonstrated field service competency

    Most distributors are standard resellers. Applied Control's Impact Partner status means: - Better product access — faster availability on non-stock items - Higher technical support access from Emerson factory teams - Training resources that non-Impact Partners don't receive

    When a competitor says "we also sell Fisher valves" — ask whether they're an Emerson Impact Partner. The answer usually tells you whether they actually have engineering capability or whether they're just a pass-through order desk.
