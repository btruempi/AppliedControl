    Glossary of Valve Automation Terms

    This glossary covers key terms from the Applied Control Valve Automation Mastery textbook. Terms are defined following ISO 11179 metadata registry principles: each definition is precise, non-circular, and written from the perspective of a practitioner in valve automation and process control.

    Use Ctrl+F (or the search icon in the navigation) to find any term quickly.

    A

    Actuator A device that converts an energy source (pneumatic pressure, electrical current, hydraulic pressure) into mechanical motion to position a control valve. The actuator is the muscle of the control valve assembly.

    AMS Device Manager Emerson's asset management software platform that collects diagnostic data from HART and FOUNDATION Fieldbus devices across a plant, provides alert management, and generates maintenance work orders. The primary tool for managing FIELDVUE DVC6200 positioner diagnostics.

    ANSI Class (Pressure Class) A standardized pressure-temperature rating system defined by ASME B16.34 for valve body flanges. Classes include 150, 300, 600, 900, 1500, and 2500, with higher numbers indicating higher maximum allowable working pressures.

    Anti-cavitation trim Valve trim designed to prevent cavitation by dropping pressure in multiple stages, keeping local pressure above vapor pressure throughout the flow path. The Fisher Cavitrol III is the standard anti-cavitation trim for Fisher ED and similar globe valve bodies.

    Authority (valve) The ratio of valve pressure drop at full flow to total system pressure drop at full flow. High authority (β > 0.25) means the installed flow characteristic approximates the inherent characteristic.

    B

    Back-pressure regulator A self-contained pressure control device that maintains a set pressure on its upstream side by venting or bypassing excess flow downstream.

    Bernoulli's equation The energy conservation equation for steady, incompressible, inviscid flow stating that the sum of static pressure, dynamic pressure (½ρv²), and hydrostatic head is constant along a streamline. Foundational to understanding pressure profiles through control valves.

    Body (valve) The primary pressure-containing element of a control valve, housing the trim, flow passages, and end connections. The body material and pressure class define the valve's operating envelope.

    Bonnet The top closure of a globe valve body that houses the stem, packing, and (in extended-bonnet designs) an insulating column for high-temperature or cryogenic service.

    Bubble-tight shutoff (Class VI) The highest leakage class per ANSI FCI 70-2. Achieves virtually zero leakage under test conditions. Requires soft seats (PTFE, elastomer) and is specified for toxic, cryogenic, or other critical isolation services.

    Butterfly valve A quarter-turn valve using a disk rotating on a central shaft to control flow. Types include concentric (wafer), high-performance (double-offset), and triple-offset. Higher-offset designs provide better shutoff and throttling control.

    C

    Cage A cylindrical trim component in globe valves that guides the plug, defines the flow characteristic through window openings, and can incorporate noise attenuation or anti-cavitation features. The Fisher ED uses cage-guided trim.

    Cage-guided trim A valve trim design where the plug is centered and guided by the cage throughout its travel, providing excellent stability and precise positioning. Standard design in the Fisher ED control valve.

    Cascade control A control architecture where the output of an outer (primary) controller sets the setpoint of an inner (secondary) controller. The inner loop is faster and rejects disturbances before they reach the outer loop.

    Cavitation The formation and rapid collapse of vapor bubbles in a liquid when local pressure drops below vapor pressure and then recovers. Bubble collapse generates shock waves that erode valve trim and create broadband noise.

    Cavitation index (sigma, σ) A dimensionless number quantifying cavitation risk: σ = (P₂ − Pᵥ)/ΔP. Lower values indicate greater cavitation severity. When σ falls below the inception sigma (σᵢ) for the valve, cavitation is occurring.

    Cavitrol III Fisher's proprietary multi-stage anti-cavitation trim. Available in 2-stage and 3-stage configurations. Drops pressure incrementally to keep local pressure above vapor pressure through the entire flow path.

    cGMP (Current Good Manufacturing Practice) FDA regulations governing pharmaceutical manufacturing, requiring validated processes, documented procedures, and electronic audit trails. Control valve diagnostics with FIELDVUE DVC6200 support cGMP compliance.

    CHARM (Characterized I/O Module) Emerson's single-channel I/O card for DeltaV. CHARMs enable electronic marshalling — I/O type is configured in software, eliminating the need to pull a field wire when I/O assignment changes.

    Choked flow The condition in which increasing differential pressure across a valve produces no additional flow. Occurs when the velocity at the vena contracta reaches the local speed of sound. Maximum flow is then a function of upstream conditions only.

    CIS (Concept Impact Score) In the Applied Control learning graph, the CIS measures how many other concepts depend (directly or indirectly) on a given concept. High-CIS concepts are foundational — mastering them enables mastering many downstream topics. Calculated recursively: CIS(x) = 1 + Σ CIS(d).

    Control valve A variable-orifice device that regulates fluid flow in a process control loop by varying the flow area through the valve. The final control element in a feedback control loop.

    Controller output (CO) The signal generated by the controller and sent to the final element (valve). In a standard loop, 4–20 mA maps to 0–100% valve travel.

    Cv (flow coefficient) The number of US gallons per minute of water (at 60°F) that flows through a valve at a pressure drop of 1 PSID. The universal sizing parameter for control valves.

    D

    Dead time The time delay between a change in controller output (manipulated variable) and the first detectable response in the process variable. Dead time degrades control loop performance and reduces achievable closed-loop bandwidth.

    DeltaV DCS Emerson's flagship Distributed Control System. Features CHARMs electronic marshalling, integrated HART diagnostics, built-in function block programming (IEC 61131-3), and native integration with FIELDVUE positioners and Rosemount transmitters.

    DeltaV SIS Emerson's Safety Instrumented System logic solver, integrated with the DeltaV platform. IEC 61508-certified logic solver for Safety Instrumented Functions rated to SIL 2 and SIL 3.

    Derivative (D) action Controller output contribution proportional to the rate of change of the error signal. Anticipates process movement and pre-corrects; amplifies sensor noise and is often set to zero for noisy measurements.

    ΔP (differential pressure) The difference in pressure between upstream (P₁) and downstream (P₂) of a valve. ΔP drives flow; higher ΔP = higher flow for the same Cv.

    DVC6200 (FIELDVUE Digital Valve Controller) Fisher's flagship intelligent positioner. Provides precise valve position control, HART communication, full valve signature, friction trending, travel deviation alerting, and partial stroke testing capability.

    DVC2000 (FIELDVUE Digital Valve Controller) Fisher's entry-level smart positioner. Provides HART communication and basic diagnostics without full signature analysis capability.

    E

    ED valve (Fisher) Fisher's most versatile general-purpose control valve. Single-port globe body, cage-guided trim, available in 1/2" to 20", ANSI 150 to 2500, with a full range of trim options including Cavitrol III and WhisperFlo.

    Equal-percentage characteristic A flow characteristic in which equal increments of valve travel produce equal percentage increases in Cv. Formula: Cv = Cv_max × R^(t-1), where R is rangeability and t is fractional travel. Provides constant process gain across a wide flow range. Most common characteristic for throttling service.

    Error signal In a feedback control loop, the difference between setpoint (SP) and process variable (PV): e = SP − PV. The controller drives error toward zero.

    EZ valve (Fisher) A cost-optimized single-port globe valve with stem-guided trim for lower-pressure general service. Size range ¾" to 6", ANSI 150 to 300. Used where cage-guided precision is not required.

    F

    Fail-safe action The valve position reached on loss of control signal or instrument air supply. Selection is based on process safety analysis: fail-open for cooling valves, fail-closed for fuel or feed valves.

    FIELDVUE Fisher's brand name for intelligent valve controllers (positioners) with digital communication and diagnostic capabilities. The DVC6200 is the flagship FIELDVUE product.

    FL (liquid pressure recovery factor) A dimensionless factor (0 to 1) characterizing how much pressure recovers between the vena contracta and the valve outlet. Higher FL means less pressure recovery, and more resistance to cavitation for a given service condition. Globe valves have higher FL (0.85–0.95) than ball valves (0.55–0.75).

    Flashing A two-phase flow condition in which downstream pressure (P₂) is below vapor pressure (Pᵥ). Unlike cavitation, vapor bubbles formed at the vena contracta do not collapse but persist through the valve outlet as vapor phase.

    Flow characteristic The relationship between valve travel and flow capacity (Cv). Inherent characteristics (linear, equal-percentage, quick-opening) describe lab behavior; installed characteristics describe actual process behavior accounting for system pressure changes with flow.

    FOUNDATION Fieldbus A digital process communication protocol for process instrumentation. Provides bi-directional digital communication, distributed control with function blocks in field devices, and eliminates analog-to-digital conversion.

    Function block In DeltaV and IEC 61131-3 programming, a reusable control algorithm module. Standard blocks include PID, AI (analog input), AO (analog output), and dozens of advanced control functions.

    G

    Globe valve A linear-motion valve with fluid flowing through a circular seat port, controlled by a plug or disk attached to a stem. The most common control valve style for throttling service. Named for the historically spherical body shape.

    H

    HART protocol Highway Addressable Remote Transducer. A digital communication standard that rides on the standard 4–20 mA analog signal. HART allows configuration, calibration, diagnostics, and process variables to be read digitally without replacing the analog infrastructure.

    High-performance butterfly valve (HPBV) A butterfly valve with a double-eccentric (double-offset) disk design that provides better shutoff (Class III–IV) and throttling range than concentric wafer butterflies. Fisher 8580 series.

    HP series (Fisher) Fisher's high-pressure globe valve series. Available in ANSI 600 to 4500 pressure classes for severe service: high-pressure steam conditioning, gas letdown, supercritical service.

    I

    IEC 60534 International standard for control valve flow capacity sizing. Defines sizing equations for liquid, gas, and steam service, including correction factors for viscosity, piping geometry, two-phase flow, and noise.

    IEC 61508 International standard for functional safety of electrical/electronic/programmable electronic systems. Defines SIL levels and the safety lifecycle for safety-critical systems.

    IEC 61511 International standard for functional safety of safety instrumented systems in the process industry. Defines SIL targets, LOPA methodology, and proof testing requirements. Specifies how IEC 61508-certified equipment (DeltaV SIS, DVC6200) is applied in process plants.

    Integral (I) action Controller output contribution proportional to the accumulated error over time. Eliminates steady-state offset. Can cause integral windup if the valve saturates.

    Instrument air Clean, dry, oil-free compressed air used to power pneumatic actuators and instruments. Typical plant supply: 80–120 PSIG; instrument headers: 18–35 PSIG.

    Installed characteristic The actual relationship between valve travel and flow in a piping system, accounting for the fact that system pressure drop changes with flow rate. Differs from the inherent (lab-measured) characteristic unless valve authority is high.

    K

    Kv The metric equivalent of Cv: the flow rate in m³/h of water at 1 bar ΔP. Conversion: Cv = 1.156 × Kv. Used in IEC documents; Cv is used in ISA and Fisher sizing software.

    L

    Leakage class A classification per ANSI FCI 70-2 defining the maximum allowable leak rate through a closed control valve. Classes range from Class I (no test required) to Class VI (bubble-tight). Fisher globe valves standard: Class IV (0.01% of rated Cv).

    Linear characteristic A flow characteristic in which Cv is directly proportional to valve travel: Cv = Cv_max × t. Best when valve pressure drop is constant (high valve authority).

    LOPA (Layer of Protection Analysis) A risk assessment method used to determine SIL targets. Each independent protection layer (IPL) reduces risk by a factor of 10–100. The required SIL is determined by how much risk reduction remains to be provided by the Safety Instrumented Function.

    M

    Manipulated variable (MV) The process variable that the controller adjusts through the final element (valve) to bring the controlled variable to setpoint. In a level control loop, MV is typically the outlet flow rate.

    Micro Motion Emerson's brand of Coriolis mass flow and density meters. Measures mass flow directly (not volumetrically) using the Coriolis effect on vibrating flow tubes. Used for custody transfer, batch control, and two-phase density measurement.

    Multi-stage pressure drop A valve trim design philosophy in which the total pressure drop is divided across multiple stages, preventing any single stage from creating conditions for cavitation or excessive noise. Implemented in Cavitrol III, WhisperFlo, and similar Fisher trims.

    N

    NACE MR0175/ISO 15156 The material requirement standard for equipment used in H₂S-containing (sour) service in oil and gas. Specifies maximum hardness limits and approved alloys to prevent sulfide stress cracking. Applied Control specifies NACE-compliant materials for sour service applications.

    Noise attenuation trim Valve trim designed to reduce aerodynamic noise in gas or steam service by dividing the flow into many small-diameter jets, reducing jet velocity and noise generation. Fisher WhisperFlo and WhisperTrim are standard noise attenuation trims.

    P

    Packing The sealing material around the valve stem that prevents process fluid from escaping to atmosphere. Common packing types: PTFE (low friction, chemical resistance), graphite (high temperature), live-loaded spring packing (low fugitive emissions, LDAR compliance).

    Partial stroke test (PST) A safety valve proof test method in which the valve is moved a partial stroke (typically 10–15%) while remaining in service to verify mechanical integrity. Performed by the DVC6200 without requiring process shutdown. Required documentation for SIL 2/SIL 3 safety instrumented functions under IEC 61511.

    PFDavg (Average Probability of Failure on Demand) The statistical measure used to determine SIL: the probability that a SIF will fail to respond when demanded. Lower PFDavg = higher SIL. Calculated from component failure rates, diagnostic coverage, and proof test intervals.

    PID controller A feedback controller combining three modes: Proportional (present error), Integral (accumulated error), Derivative (rate of change of error). The most common control algorithm in industrial process control.

    Plug The moving trim element in a globe valve that controls flow area by varying its position relative to the seat ring. The plug profile determines the flow characteristic in non-cage-guided designs.

    Positioner A local feedback controller mounted on the valve actuator that compares commanded position to actual stem position and modulates actuating pressure to eliminate position error. Eliminates the effect of packing friction and supply pressure variation on valve position.

    Process variable (PV) The measured quantity to be controlled in a control loop (e.g., temperature, pressure, flow, level).

    Proportional (P) action Controller output contribution directly proportional to current error. Faster response than integral, but leaves a steady-state offset.

    Q

    Quick-opening characteristic A flow characteristic in which most Cv increase occurs at low travel positions. Formula: Cv ≈ Cv_max × √t. Best for on/off service where maximum flow is needed immediately on opening.

    R

    Rangeability The ratio of maximum to minimum controllable Cv: R = Cv_max/Cv_min. Fisher ED globe: 50:1; Fisher Vee-Ball: 100:1; concentric butterfly: 10:1.

    Regulator (self-contained) A pressure control device that uses the controlled pressure to directly actuate the valve without requiring an external power source. Used for pressure reduction and back-pressure control at moderate accuracy requirements.

    Reynolds number (Re) A dimensionless number characterizing flow regime: Re = ρvD/μ. Re < 2,000: laminar; Re > 4,000: turbulent. Standard Cv sizing tables assume turbulent flow; viscosity corrections are required for laminar or transitional service.

    Rosemount Emerson's instrumentation brand. Key products: Rosemount 3051 pressure transmitter (the industry standard), Rosemount 8800 vortex flowmeter, Rosemount 5408 radar level transmitter, Rosemount 644 temperature transmitter.

    S

    Safety Instrumented Function (SIF) A specific safety function that is implemented using a safety instrumented system (SIS). A SIF has a defined safe state, a demand rate, and a SIL target.

    Safety Instrumented System (SIS) An instrumented system designed to bring a process to a safe state when predetermined conditions are violated. Consists of sensors, logic solver, and final elements (valves). Must be designed and maintained to IEC 61511 requirements.

    Safety Integrity Level (SIL) A discrete level (1, 2, 3, or 4) corresponding to a range of safety integrity for a safety function. SIL 1 (PFDavg 0.1–0.01) through SIL 4 (PFDavg 0.0001–0.00001). Most process industry applications target SIL 2 or SIL 3.

    Seat (valve) The fixed trim surface against which the plug seals when the valve is closed. Seat material affects leakage class, temperature rating, and corrosion resistance.

    Setpoint (SP) The desired value of the process variable. Set by the operator or an outer control loop in cascade control.

    SIL (see Safety Integrity Level)

    Sigma (see Cavitation Index)

    T

    TCO (Total Cost of Ownership) The lifetime cost of an asset including purchase, installation, maintenance, unplanned downtime, energy, and end-of-life. TCO analysis typically favors Fisher/Applied Control over lower-cost alternatives when unplanned downtime cost is included.

    Throttling service Valve service in which the valve is required to modulate flow continuously at positions between fully closed and fully open. Requires stable, predictable flow characteristics and resistance to wear.

    Trim The internal components of a control valve that are wetted by the process fluid and determine flow capacity, characteristic, shutoff, noise, and cavitation behavior: plug, seat ring, cage, stem.

    Triple-offset butterfly valve A butterfly valve with three geometric offsets that create a cone-shaped seating geometry, eliminating friction during opening and closing. Achieves Class V/VI shutoff and is suitable for high-pressure, high-temperature service.

    V

    Valve authority (see Authority)

    Valve signature The stroke-by-stroke record of actuating pressure vs. stem travel (or vice versa) produced by a FIELDVUE DVC6200 during a stroke test. The signature reveals packing friction (hysteresis band width), seat condition, spring rate, and actuator health.

    ValveLink Emerson's valve sizing and management software. Performs Cv sizing per IEC 60534, noise calculations per IEC 60534-8, cavitation analysis, actuator sizing, and generates sizing reports. Integrates with FIELDVUE diagnostics.

    Vapor pressure (Pᵥ) The pressure at which a liquid begins to vaporize at a given temperature. When valve pressure drops below vapor pressure, cavitation (if P₂ > Pᵥ) or flashing (if P₂ < Pᵥ) occurs.

    Vee-Ball (Fisher) Fisher's V-notch segmented ball valve series. The V-notch creates an inherent equal-percentage characteristic. Excellent for slurry, solids-laden, and large-flow liquid service. Rangeability 100:1.

    Vena contracta The cross-sectional point of minimum flow area (and maximum velocity/minimum pressure) downstream of a valve orifice. Pressure at the vena contracta determines whether cavitation or flashing will occur.

    W

    WhisperFlo / WhisperTrim Fisher's noise attenuation trim family for gas and steam service. Multiple small orifices divide a single high-velocity jet into many small jets, dramatically reducing aerodynamic noise per IEC 60534-8-3.

    WirelessHART A wireless mesh network protocol for process instrumentation (IEC 62591). Built on HART application layer; enables installation of transmitters without wiring. Emerson Smart Wireless products include the 702/wireless THUM adapter.
