    Chapter 17: Life Sciences, Water & HVAC

    Learning Objectives

    • Remember: Identify the two FDA regulations most relevant to control valve and instrumentation documentation in pharmaceutical manufacturing
    • Understand: Explain why sanitary design (3-A standards) is required for food, beverage, and pharmaceutical process equipment, and what specific design features it mandates
    • Apply: Specify the correct Fisher valve, actuator, and positioner for a WFI (Water for Injection) distribution loop that requires cGMP compliance and full electronic audit trail
    • Analyze: Compare the measurement and control requirements of a municipal water treatment plant to a pharmaceutical purified water system, and identify the key differences
    • Evaluate: Identify the three most important Applied Control differentiators for a pharmaceutical customer conducting a vendor qualification audit

    17.1 Life Sciences — Pharmaceutical and Biotech

    Pharmaceutical and biotechnology manufacturing is among the most regulated industries in the world. Every piece of process equipment — including control valves and transmitters — must meet FDA requirements for hygiene, cleanability, and documentation.

    Regulatory Framework

    FDA 21 CFR Part 11 — Electronic Records and Signatures: Any electronic record that substitutes for a paper record in a regulated process must meet 21 CFR Part 11 requirements: - Audit trail: Complete record of all configuration changes, calibration events, and alarm acknowledgments — with timestamp and user identity - Access control: Role-based access; operators cannot change valve configuration; only qualified engineers can - Data integrity: Records cannot be modified without creating a new record with the change documented

    FDA 21 CFR Part 210/211 — Current Good Manufacturing Practice (cGMP): cGMP defines the manufacturing practices required for drug product production. For process equipment: - Equipment must be designed and installed to facilitate cleaning and maintenance - Equipment qualification (IQ/OQ/PQ) must be documented before use in production - Calibration of all measuring instruments must be documented and traceable to NIST

    The DeltaV + DVC6200 documentation advantage: DeltaV's historian records every process value, alarm, and operator action with timestamp — meeting 21 CFR Part 11 audit trail requirements. The DVC6200 records all configuration changes via HART audit trail in AMS Device Manager. This electronic documentation package significantly reduces the burden of paper-based batch record systems.

    Sanitary Design — 3-A Standards

    3-A Sanitary Standards (defined by 3-A SSI — Sanitary Standards Inc.) specify design requirements for food, beverage, and dairy equipment that must be cleanable to microbiological standards:

    • No dead legs: All wetted surfaces must drain completely; no horizontal dead legs longer than 3× the pipe diameter
    • Surface finish: Wetted surfaces 32 Ra μin (0.8 μm Ra) or better — typically electropolished 316L stainless steel
    • No crevices: All connections use clamp-type tri-clamp (sanitary) connections — no threaded fittings with crevice corrosion sites
    • Drainability: All vessels and piping slope to drain; no puddles
    • Cleanability: Equipment must withstand CIP (Clean-in-Place) with hot caustic and hot acid; SIP (Steam-in-Place) at 121°C minimum for sterilization

    Fisher sanitary valve offerings: - Fisher V-Series sanitary ball valves: 3-A certified, tri-clamp ends, electropolished 316L, EPDM or silicone seats, full-bore design - Fisher ES butterfly valve: Sanitary elastomer seat, tri-clamp, for large-bore low-pressure service - DVC6200 on sanitary valves: Standard DVC6200 with sanitary mount kit; HART audit trail meets 21 CFR Part 11

    Water for Injection (WFI) Systems

    WFI is the highest-purity water used in pharmaceutical manufacturing — used for final product formulation, equipment rinsing, and injectables. WFI distribution loops typically circulate at 80°C or higher to prevent microbial growth.

    WFI control requirements: - Temperature: 80–85°C maintained continuously (monitored by Rosemount RTDs at multiple points) - Flow: Minimum circulation velocity to prevent biofilm formation (>1 m/s) - Control valves: Sanitary design, zero dead legs, 316L electropolished, DVC6200 with HART audit

    Point-of-use (POU) valves: At each process connection point, a diaphragm valve or sanitary ball valve controls WFI delivery. Fisher sanitary diaphragm valves (PTFE-lined body, EPDM diaphragm) are common for these applications.

    Equipment Qualification — IQ/OQ/PQ

    Before a control valve or DCS can be used in FDA-regulated production, it must be qualified:

    Phase Stands for What is documented
    IQ (Installation Qualification) Installation Qualification Valve model, tag, serial number, materials of construction, connection sizes match the design specification
    OQ (Operational Qualification) Operational Qualification Valve strokes correctly; DVC6200 calibration verified; HART communication confirmed; fail-safe action confirmed
    PQ (Performance Qualification) Performance Qualification The control loop performs within specification during production — temperature held within ±0.5°C, flow controlled within ±1%

    Applied Control can prepare the IQ/OQ documentation package as part of a project delivery — a significant differentiator for pharmaceutical customers who spend enormous effort on qualification documentation.

    17.2 Food and Beverage

    Food and beverage manufacturing shares many requirements with pharmaceutical — sanitary design, CIP/SIP compatibility, FDA compliance — but with somewhat less stringent documentation requirements (no IQ/OQ/PQ for most food processes).

    Key segments and Applied Control opportunities:

    Dairy

    • Pasteurization control: Precise temperature control at 161°F/72°C (HTST) or 280°F/138°C (UHT); Fisher sanitary globe or butterfly valve; Rosemount temperature with sanitary tri-clamp connection
    • Cream separation: Centrifuge control; Rosemount magnetic flow for volume measurement
    • CIP systems: Caustic and acid cleaning solutions at high temperature; EPDM seats for caustic compatibility; silicone seats for acid compatibility

    Beverage (Beer, Soft Drinks, Juice)

    • Carbonation control: CO₂ dosing; precise pressure control; Rosemount pressure transmitter; Fisher EZ or ED
    • Blending: Multi-stream ratio control; Micro Motion Coriolis for precise ingredient dosing by mass
    • Fermentation temperature: Glycol cooling control; Fisher EZ with 667 (fail-closed cooling = fail-open glycol) or 657 (fail-open cooling)

    Brewing — Unique Requirements

    Brewing involves biological processes (yeast fermentation) that are highly sensitive to temperature and oxygen exposure. DeltaV batch control is ideal for recipe-based brewing where each batch step (mash temperature ramp, yeast pitch temperature, fermentation temperature profile) is defined in a recipe and executed consistently across hundreds of batches.

    17.3 Water and Wastewater

    Municipal Water Treatment

    Water treatment plants process raw surface water or groundwater into potable drinking water. Key process steps and Applied Control opportunities:

    Coagulation and flocculation: - Chemical dosing (alum, polymer): Precise flow control; Rosemount magnetic flowmeter (Coriolis for smaller flows); Fisher EZ or EW globe valve - pH control: Rosemount pH transmitter; lime or CO₂ dosing valve

    Filtration: - Filter backwash: High-flow, brief-duration valves; Fisher HP butterfly for large-bore backwash headers - Filter influent/effluent control: Butterfly valves with electric actuators (no instrument air in many water plants)

    Disinfection: - Chlorination: Chlorine gas or sodium hypochlorite dosing; corrosion-resistant trim (Hastelloy or titanium wetted parts); Rosemount flow measurement - UV disinfection: No chemical dosing; simpler control

    Distribution pump control: - Variable speed drives (VFDs) on main distribution pumps; Rosemount pressure transmitters for system pressure feedback; Fisher control valves on pressure zones and PRV stations

    Wastewater Treatment

    Primary treatment (screening and settling): No significant control valve content.

    Secondary treatment (biological — activated sludge): - Aeration control: Dissolved oxygen measurement (Rosemount optical DO sensor); modulating air blower VFD or butterfly valve to maintain DO at 2 mg/L setpoint - Return activated sludge (RAS) flow: Fisher V-notch Vee-Ball or eccentric plug valve (handles sludge solids) - Waste activated sludge (WAS): Fisher Vee-Ball; flow measurement by Rosemount magnetic meter

    Sludge treatment (anaerobic digester): - Digester temperature: 95–100°F (mesophilic); heat exchanger with Fisher EZ temperature control - Biogas pressure: Self-regulating Fisher 627 regulator for biogas supply to boilers - Biogas flow: Thermal mass flowmeter (low pressure, variable composition)

    Applied Control message for water/wastewater: "We understand the constraints — no instrument air at most sites, tight capital budgets, and operators who maintain equipment rather than instrumentation specialists. Fisher Vee-Balls with electric actuators, Rosemount instruments with WirelessHART where wiring is cost-prohibitive, and 627 self-contained regulators for pump stations — simple, reliable, and maintainable by your staff."

    17.4 HVAC and Building Automation

    HVAC (Heating, Ventilation, Air Conditioning) is not Applied Control's core market, but represents opportunity in large industrial and campus facilities where central utility systems include steam, chilled water, and cooling tower control.

    Central plant control: - Chilled water supply temperature: Centrifugal chiller control; large butterfly valves on evaporator and condenser water circuits - Steam-to-hot water heat exchangers: Fisher EZ globe valve with 667 actuator (fail-closed); steam pressure downstream of PRV (Fisher 627 regulator) for building heat - Cooling tower basin level: Makeup water control; Fisher EZ or EW globe valve; Rosemount level measurement

    Differential pressure control for variable air volume (VAV) systems: - Building supply air pressure is maintained by variable-speed supply fans with duct static pressure feedback - Zone-level VAV boxes control individual zone temperatures with small butterfly or ball valves - Rosemount differential pressure transmitters (3051) monitor filter status and system balance

    Chapter Summary

    Life sciences manufacturing requires the highest level of documentation, hygienic design, and process control precision. Fisher sanitary valves (3-A compliant), DVC6200 with 21 CFR Part 11 audit trail, and DeltaV batch control provide the complete package for pharmaceutical and food/beverage applications. Water and wastewater treatment requires reliable, low-maintenance equipment tolerant of solids and variable flow conditions — Fisher Vee-Ball and eccentric plug valves for sludge; Rosemount magnetic meters for flow; WirelessHART for remote sites without wiring infrastructure; Fisher 627 regulators for pump station pressure control. Applied Control's breadth across all these industries — using the same core Fisher/Rosemount/DeltaV portfolio — allows a single sales engineer to address the complete utility and process control needs of a large institutional or municipal customer.

    Sales Implications

    Sales bridge — Life sciences and water/wastewater

    1. FDA qualification documentation is a significant pain. Any pharmaceutical customer who has gone through an FDA audit on a control valve or DCS system knows how much documentation is required. Applied Control providing a pre-formatted IQ/OQ documentation package for every valve and DVC6200 is a tangible service that saves the customer 10–20 hours of engineering time per installation.
    2. 3-A compliance verification is a specialty. When a food/beverage customer is under FDA inspection, every piece of equipment in their process must have documented compliance. Applied Control can provide the 3-A certification letters for Fisher sanitary valves — something a general distributor cannot.
    3. Water/wastewater is a volume market. Individual valve prices are low, but a large water treatment plant has hundreds of control points. A framework agreement for Rosemount instrumentation and Fisher V-series valves with a regional water authority provides years of repeatable business.
    4. Energy efficiency is the water utility entry point. Most water utilities are focused on reducing energy costs (pumping is 30–40% of a water utility's operating cost). Applied Control's differential pressure measurement and pump control optimization (using Rosemount transmitters and DeltaV PID loops to reduce pump energy by 15–25%) positions the company as an energy partner, not just an equipment supplier.
