    Chapter 6: Isolation, Choke & Specialty Valves

    Learning Objectives

    • Remember: Identify the four main isolation valve types and state the primary failure mode of each
    • Understand: Explain why a gate valve should never be used for throttling and what happens to the trim when it is
    • Apply: Select the correct isolation or choke valve type for a given service condition (pressure, temperature, fluid type, cycle frequency)
    • Analyze: Compare the sealing mechanisms of soft-seat vs. metal-seat ball valves and determine which applies in a high-temperature hydrogen service
    • Evaluate: Justify the selection of a Fisher V150 Vee-Ball over a gate valve for a produced water isolation application with frequent cycling

    6.1 Isolation vs. Control

    Previous chapters covered control valves — valves designed to throttle flow continuously. This chapter covers the other major valve category: isolation valves, which are designed to be either fully open or fully closed, providing a block between sections of piping.

    Characteristic Control valve Isolation valve
    Normal operating position Partially open (throttling) Fully open or fully closed
    Trim design Profiled for flow characteristic Optimized for tight shutoff and low pressure drop
    Actuator type Pneumatic, modulating On/off pneumatic, electric, or manual
    Cycle frequency Continuous (thousands/year) Infrequent (annual maintenance, emergency)
    Leakage class Class IV–VI (controlled leakage) Class VI or metal-to-metal (near-zero)

    6.2 Gate Valves

    The gate valve is the oldest and most common isolation valve in industrial piping. A flat or wedge-shaped gate (disc) moves perpendicular to the flow path to open or close.

    Construction: - Gate: solid wedge, flexible wedge, or split wedge - Seats: integral body seats or replaceable seat rings - Stem: rising stem (visible position indicator) or non-rising (enclosed in gland)

    Operating characteristics: - Fully open: gate retracts completely out of flow path → very low pressure drop (Cv nearly equal to full-bore pipe) - Fully closed: wedge seats against tapered body seats → metal-to-metal seal

    Why gate valves must NOT throttle:

    When a gate valve is operated in a partially open position, the high-velocity flow across the narrow gate opening causes intense erosion of the gate and seat faces. Within a relatively short time, the seating surfaces are wire-drawn (grooved), and the valve can no longer seal. A gate valve operated at 10% open will typically fail to seal within months in a moderate-velocity liquid service.

    Selection criteria for gate valves: - Large-diameter piping where pressure drop and cost matter (2" and larger) - Infrequent operation — annual isolation for maintenance, emergency block - Services not requiring frequent cycling - Applications where a rising stem indicates position visually

    Fisher/Emerson equivalent: Gate valves are commodity items. Applied Control focuses on the actuator and automation package for gate valve motorization — EIM electric actuators for large automated gate valves.

    6.3 Globe Valves (Isolation Pattern)

    A globe valve in isolation service uses the same body as a control valve but with a full-bore plug and seat designed for maximum flow and tight shutoff rather than profiled flow characteristic.

    Advantages over gate valves: - Better sealing repeatability - Easier to repair (plug and seat accessible through bonnet) - Can be used for moderate throttling without damage - Better suited to high-pressure and high-temperature service

    Applications: Steam isolation, high-pressure gas block valves, pump and compressor block/bypass valves.

    6.4 Ball Valves

    A ball valve uses a spherical ball with a through-bore. A quarter turn (90°) rotates the bore from aligned with the pipe (open) to perpendicular (closed). Ball valves are the dominant isolation valve in sizes from ½" to 12" because of their:

    • Simple quarter-turn operation (fast open/close)
    • Excellent sealing with soft seats
    • Low torque requirement
    • Full-bore design (no pressure drop restriction)
    • Suitability for automation

    Soft Seat vs. Metal Seat

    Characteristic Soft seat (PTFE/PEEK) Metal seat (316SS, Stellite)
    Sealing ANSI Class VI — essentially zero leakage Class IV–V — some leakage at elevated temperature
    Temperature limit 200°C (PTFE), 260°C (PEEK) Up to 538°C
    Service Ambient liquids, gases, clean service Steam, high-temperature HC, abrasive service
    Cycle life High (PTFE conforms and self-repairs) Moderate (metal seats wear with particles)

    Trunnion-mounted vs. floating ball:

    • Floating ball: Ball is held only by the seats; process pressure forces ball against downstream seat. Good up to Class 600. Seat loading increases with pressure.
    • Trunnion-mounted: Ball is pinned top and bottom to the body. Seats are spring-loaded and move toward the ball. Required for Class 900+ and large diameters (>8") where floating ball would over-load the seats.

    6.5 Fisher V150/V200 Vee-Ball Valves

    The Fisher Vee-Ball is Applied Control's primary ball valve for services requiring moderate throttling capability combined with excellent shutoff. The Vee-Ball is NOT a standard ball valve — its V-notch ball provides a flow characteristic more like a control valve than an on/off isolation valve.

    Why the Vee-Ball Wins in Difficult Services

    Feature Benefit
    V-notch trim Equal-percentage characteristic — good throttle and tight shutoff from same valve
    Shearing action V-notch shears fibrous material, slurries, and solids as it closes — no plugging
    Class IV–V shutoff Better than most globe control valves; suitable for isolation on most services
    High Cv Large flow capacity for a given pipe size vs. globe valve
    Quarter-turn Fast operation; simple spring-return pneumatic actuator

    Vee-Ball Applications

    • Produced water (oil & gas): Solids and sand shear cleanly; no plugging with V-notch
    • Pulp and paper: Fiber content in liquid shears at the V-notch
    • Water and wastewater: Large flow rates, solids handling
    • General chemical: Where moderate throttling plus isolation is needed
    • Cooling water headers: Large-bore with high Cv

    V150 vs. V200

    Model Body style Size range Pressure class
    V150 Flanged 1"–12" Class 150/300
    V200 Flanged 1"–12" Class 150/300
    V300 High performance (characterized) 1"–8" Class 150–600

    The V150 is the standard offering; specify the V300 when tighter control characteristic is needed at higher pressure.

    6.6 Butterfly Valves

    A butterfly valve uses a disc that rotates 90° around a central axis within the valve body. In the open position, the disc is parallel to flow (minimal pressure drop); in the closed position, it blocks flow.

    Types:

    Type Description Sealing Application
    Wafer Clamped between flanges Soft seat (EPDM, PTFE) Low-pressure water, HVAC
    Lug Threaded inserts for dead-end isolation Soft or metal seat Moderate pressure piping
    Double offset (high-performance) Disc offset from shaft — reduces seat wear on open/close PTFE or metal Process plant, moderate throttling
    Triple offset (TOV) Disc, shaft, and cone axis all offset — zero friction seating Metal-to-metal High temp/pressure fire-safe service

    Fisher High Performance Butterfly Valve (HPBV): The double-offset design provides a cam action that lifts the disc off the seat before rotation, eliminating the scrubbing action that destroys soft seats in conventional butterflies. Suitable for throttling service in sizes 3"–72".

    Limitation: Butterfly valves place the disc in the flow stream at all positions — creating turbulence and vibration in partial-open positions. Not suitable for fine throttling control. The disc also prevents pigging of the pipeline.

    6.7 Plug Valves

    A plug valve uses a cylindrical or conical plug with a port through it. Quarter-turn operation, like a ball valve, but with a larger seating surface. Common in natural gas distribution (lubricated plug valves) and in chemical service with sleeved PTFE seats.

    Eccentric plug valves (Fisher 8580): The plug rotates eccentrically, lifting off the seat before rotating — similar to the double-offset butterfly principle. Provides tight shutoff in wastewater, slurry, and abrasive service where a ball valve would abrade.

    6.8 Choke Valves — Severe Pressure Drop

    A choke valve is a specialized control/isolation valve designed to handle extremely high pressure drops — often from wellhead pressures of 5,000–15,000 PSIG down to separator pressures of 50–200 PSIG. In oil and gas production, the choke valve is the critical flow control element at the wellhead.

    The Choke Valve Problem

    A 10,000 PSIG drop across a small valve causes: - Extreme velocity at the vena contracta → erosion - Flashing and cavitation if liquid drops below vapor pressure - Sand and solids carried in production fluids accelerate erosion - Acoustic noise from gas expansion can exceed 130 dB - Vibration that fatigues body, bonnet, and downstream piping

    Fisher Choke Valve Solutions

    Fisher MegaStream: Bean/orifice style adjustable choke for wellhead service. The "bean" is a removable, replaceable orifice insert sized for the desired flow restriction. Tungsten carbide or ceramic inserts for erosion resistance.

    Fisher ED Severe Service (with Cavitrol trim): For liquid-heavy well streams where cavitation is the primary concern. Cavitrol IV staged-restriction trim reduces velocity at each stage, keeping pressure above vapor pressure until the final stage where flash is controlled.

    Key selection parameters for choke valves: - Wellhead shut-in pressure (SITP) - Flowing wellhead pressure (FWHP) - Required flow rate at design conditions - Gas-to-oil ratio (GOR) — determines flash/choke behavior - Sand content — determines erosion trim material (Stellite, tungsten carbide, ceramic) - Required Cv range (fixed orifice vs. adjustable needle/cage)

    6.9 Check Valves

    A check valve allows flow in one direction only — it opens under forward flow and closes automatically when flow reverses. Not controlled by an actuator; the check mechanism is driven by differential pressure.

    Types:

    Type Operation Application
    Swing check Hinged disc swings open Low-velocity liquid lines; horizontal installation
    Lift check Piston/ball lifts off seat High-pressure gas; vertical upflow
    Wafer check Spring-loaded dual disc Compact; works in any orientation; fast closure
    Axial flow (nozzle) check Streamlined spring-loaded cone Minimum pressure drop; pump discharge
    Ball check Ball rolls off seat under forward flow Slurry, viscous fluids

    Slam vs. non-slam: Swing checks can slam shut on flow reversal, creating water hammer. Wafer dual-disc checks and axial checks close quickly and gently (non-slam) — preferred on pump discharge where water hammer is a concern.

    Chapter Summary

    Isolation valves provide the block function — fully open or fully closed — that control valves cannot provide reliably. Gate valves dominate large-diameter infrequent-cycle service but must never throttle. Ball valves dominate ½"–12" service because of their quarter-turn speed, low torque, and excellent sealing. The Fisher Vee-Ball extends ball valve technology into moderate throttling service with a V-notch that also handles solids and fibrous materials without plugging. Butterfly valves cover large-diameter low-cost service but are limited in throttling precision. Choke valves address the most severe application in process industries — high-wellhead-pressure letdown with sand erosion and flashing — requiring tungsten carbide trim and staged pressure reduction. Check valves provide passive backflow prevention; non-slam designs prevent water hammer on pump discharge.

    Quiz

    Question 1 — Remember

    A gate valve is found partially open (about 15% open) on a high-velocity water line. The maintenance record shows it has been operated this way for 2 years. What is the most likely condition of the valve?

    • A) The valve is fine — gate valves handle partial opening well
    • B) The gate and seats are wire-drawn (eroded) — the valve likely cannot seal fully closed anymore
    • C) The packing has failed
    • D) The stem has corroded

    Question 2 — Understand

    Why does a triple-offset butterfly valve achieve metal-to-metal sealing without the friction wear that destroys conventional butterfly seats?

    • A) It uses a harder seat material
    • B) The disc is smaller than the bore
    • C) The three offsets create a cam action so the disc lifts off the seat before rotating — there is no sliding friction between disc and seat during operation
    • D) It uses a PTFE coating on the disc

    Question 3 — Evaluate

    A produced water pipeline handles water with 2–5% sand content at 400 GPM, 150 PSIG, 80°F. The current gate valve requires isolation 4–6 times per week and has failed to seal twice in the past year. What is the best replacement?

    • A) Fisher ED globe control valve with Class VI seat
    • B) Fisher V150 Vee-Ball with tungsten carbide seat — the V-notch shears sand without plugging, the quarter-turn suits frequent cycling, and Class IV/V shutoff is adequate for produced water service
    • C) Triple-offset butterfly valve
    • D) Larger gate valve with Stellite seats
