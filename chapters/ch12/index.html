    Chapter 12: Flow Measurement

    Learning Objectives

    • Remember: Name five distinct flow measurement technologies and state the physical principle each uses
    • Understand: Explain why a Coriolis meter measures mass flow directly while an orifice plate measures volume flow, and what additional data is needed to convert between the two
    • Apply: Select the appropriate Rosemount or Micro Motion flow meter for three different services: steam utility header, crude oil custody transfer, and municipal water
    • Analyze: Calculate the actual flow through an orifice plate when the measured ΔP is 45 inH₂O and the calibrated range is 0–100 inH₂O at 500 GPM
    • Evaluate: Justify the higher capital cost of a Micro Motion Coriolis meter over an orifice plate for a chemical batch dosing application

    12.1 Why Flow Measurement Matters

    Flow is the most frequently measured process variable in industrial plants — more flow loops exist than any other control or measurement function. The economic stakes are high:

    • Custody transfer: Revenue depends on accurate measurement of product sold or purchased
    • Mass balance: Safety and environmental compliance require knowing how much of each component enters and exits a process
    • Batch recipe: Product quality depends on precise flow dosing of reactants
    • Energy efficiency: Steam and fuel metering drives utility cost optimization
    • Environmental reporting: Flare gas, stack emissions, and wastewater discharge are regulated

    Measurement error of 1% on a pipeline carrying $10 million/year of product = $100,000/year of mismeasured revenue.

    12.2 Differential Pressure Flow — Orifice Plates and Beyond

    The differential pressure (DP) flow method is the oldest and most widespread flow measurement technology. A primary element in the pipeline creates a controlled pressure drop; the DP transmitter measures this pressure difference, and the flow computer calculates flow from the DP-flow relationship.

    The DP Flow Equation

    For an incompressible (liquid) fluid:

    $$Q = C_d \cdot A_2 \cdot \sqrt{\frac{2(P_1 - P_2)}{\rho(1 - \beta^4)}}$$

    Where: - Q = volumetric flow rate (m³/s or GPM) - Cd = discharge coefficient (0.61 for orifice plate) - A₂ = orifice bore area (m²) - β = beta ratio = d/D (orifice diameter / pipe diameter) - ρ = fluid density (kg/m³)

    Key consequence: Flow varies as √ΔP. At 25% of maximum ΔP, flow is 50% of maximum. This creates poor turndown accuracy at low flow — the ΔP at 10% flow is only 1% of the DP at full flow, near the noise floor of most transmitters.

    Orifice Plate Types

    Type Description Application
    Concentric Round hole centered in plate Clean liquids, gases, steam
    Eccentric Hole offset to bottom of pipe Slurries, dirty fluids
    Segmental Partial chord opening at bottom Heavy slurries, solids
    Quadrant edge Radius-edged bore Viscous liquids (Reynolds < 10,000)

    Rosemount 3051SFC — Integrated DP Flow Transmitter

    The Rosemount 3051SFC combines differential pressure, static pressure, and temperature measurement in one device:

    • Compensated mass flow: Uses real-time P and T to correct volumetric DP flow to actual mass flow without an external flow computer
    • Eliminates three separate transmitters (DP, pressure, temperature)
    • HART 7: Reports compensated mass flow, volumetric flow, energy flow (for steam), density, and three raw measurements simultaneously

    Annubar primary element (Rosemount 485): The Annubar is a multi-point averaging pitot tube — a bar spanning the pipe with ports at multiple radial positions that average the velocity profile across the pipe. It provides: - Lower permanent pressure loss than an orifice plate - Bidirectional measurement - Simpler installation (tap into pipe vs. removing spool) - Best for large pipe diameters (6"–120")

    12.3 Vortex Flow Measurement — Rosemount 8800

    Chapter 3 introduced the Rosemount 8800D vortex flowmeter. This chapter goes deeper into the application engineering.

    Vortex Meter Limitations

    Minimum velocity requirement: Vortex shedding only occurs above a minimum Reynolds number (Re > 10,000). Below this, vortices are irregular and the meter reads unreliably. This creates a low-flow cutoff — below ~20% of maximum flow, many vortex meters do not output a reliable signal.

    No solids tolerance: Particles can coat the bluff body sensor and blunt the vortex shedding, causing dropout. Vortex is not suitable for slurries or dirty liquids with solids above ~50 ppm.

    Vibration sensitivity: External vibration on the pipe can be misinterpreted as vortex shedding. The 8800D uses a dual-sensor approach that cancels mechanical vibration while retaining the fluid vortex signal — it is significantly more vibration-immune than single-sensor competitors.

    Vortex for Steam — The Primary Application

    Vortex is the preferred technology for steam flow because: - Steam is hot, clean, and single-phase (when properly dried) - No moving parts to corrode or scale - High Reynolds numbers in steam (Re >> 10,000 typical) - Multivariable version provides compensated mass flow for billing and energy calculations

    The Rosemount 8800D MVD (Multivariable) integrates: - Vortex sensor (velocity/volumetric flow) - Integral temperature sensor (RTD) - Integral static pressure sensor - Internal flow computer: calculates compensated steam mass flow, energy flow (BTU/hr), and condensate flow — replaces 3 separate transmitters plus an external flow computer

    12.4 Coriolis Flow Measurement — Micro Motion

    The Coriolis effect is the deflection experienced by a mass moving within a rotating reference frame. In a Coriolis flowmeter, a vibrating tube replaces the rotating reference frame:

    1. A driver causes the flow tube to vibrate at its natural frequency
    2. Fluid flowing through the tube has momentum (mass × velocity)
    3. The flowing mass resists the change in direction caused by vibration
    4. This creates a measurable twist (phase shift) between inlet and outlet sensors
    5. The phase shift is proportional to mass flow rate — directly, with no density correction required

    Why Coriolis Wins — Application Cases

    Custody transfer: The API MPMS standard Chapter 6 recognizes Coriolis as a primary standard for liquid petroleum measurement. ±0.05% accuracy with no density correction and no requirement for flow conditioning (no straight-run requirements) makes it the highest-accuracy, lowest-uncertainty liquid meter available.

    Batch dosing: In a batch reactor, each reactant must be added in precise mass quantities regardless of temperature, density, or viscosity changes during filling. Coriolis measures actual mass — when the flow computer totalizer reaches the target, the valve closes. No density or temperature correction needed; no batch variance from composition change.

    Density measurement: Real-time density from a Coriolis meter enables concentration calculation for many binary mixtures (ethanol/water, NaOH/water, acid solutions). One meter replaces a flow meter + density meter + concentration calculation system.

    12.5 Magnetic Flow Measurement

    A magnetic flowmeter (magmeter) measures the velocity of a conductive fluid by measuring the EMF induced when the fluid (acting as a conductor) moves through a magnetic field (Faraday's law):

    $$E = B \cdot D \cdot V$$

    Where E = induced EMF (mV), B = magnetic field strength, D = pipe diameter, V = fluid velocity.

    Key requirement: The fluid must be electrically conductive — minimum ~5 μS/cm. This excludes hydrocarbons and most gases.

    Advantages: - No restriction in the flow path → zero permanent pressure loss - Full-bore measurement → tolerates solids, slurries, and fibrous materials - Bidirectional measurement - Unaffected by density, viscosity, or temperature changes

    Applications: Water and wastewater, slurry, pulp and paper, chemical (aqueous), pharmaceutical (conductive fluids), mining.

    Rosemount 8750W magnetic flowmeter for water/wastewater service: Offers empty-pipe detection (alarm when pipe drains), bidirectional flow, and reduced-bore option for higher velocity.

    12.6 Ultrasonic Flow Measurement

    Ultrasonic flowmeters measure fluid velocity using the time-of-flight of ultrasonic pulses transmitted diagonally across the pipe: - Downstream pulse arrives faster (added by fluid velocity) - Upstream pulse arrives slower (opposed by fluid velocity) - Difference in transit time = velocity

    Clamp-on ultrasonic: Transducers mount externally on the pipe — no pipe penetration, no wetted parts. Ideal for large pipes (6"–60"+), non-invasive measurement on existing piping, and temporary flow verification surveys.

    Spoolpiece ultrasonic (custody grade): Transducers are in the pipe wall in precision-machined ports. Multiple path (3–6 chords) averaging corrects for velocity profile distortions. Used for high-accuracy gas custody transfer (pipeline metering stations, custody LNG).

    Rosemount 3107: Submersible clamp-on for water and wastewater; rated for buried installations.

    12.7 Thermal Mass Flow Measurement

    A thermal mass flowmeter measures the heat required to maintain a heated sensor at a constant temperature above the flowing fluid — more mass flow = more heat required = higher power draw.

    $$P = c_p \cdot \dot{m} \cdot \Delta T$$

    Applications: Low-flow gas measurement (instrument air, nitrogen, hydrogen, natural gas in small lines), flare gas monitoring.

    Rosemount 3051SFP Annubar + CDS transmitter as a combined thermal mass flow system for flare gas — measures mass flow of low-pressure mixed-composition flare gas streams for environmental reporting.

    Chapter Summary

    Flow measurement encompasses more distinct physical principles than any other process measurement category. DP with an orifice plate is universal but limited by √ΔP turndown. Vortex is the preferred steam flow technology — rugged, no moving parts, multivariable mass flow compensation. Coriolis provides the highest accuracy mass flow measurement, direct density measurement, and 100:1 turndown for custody transfer and batch applications. Magnetic meters cover conductive liquids with zero pressure drop and full solids tolerance. Ultrasonic enables non-invasive measurement on large pipes. The Rosemount and Micro Motion product families cover every flow application from water mains to crude oil custody transfer, all communicating over HART or FOUNDATION Fieldbus to AMS Device Manager and DeltaV.

    Quiz

    Question 1 — Apply

    An orifice plate flow meter is calibrated for 0–500 GPM at 0–100 inH₂O DP. The measured DP is 45 inH₂O. What is the actual flow?

    • A) 225 GPM (45% of 500)
    • B) 250 GPM (50% of 500)
    • C) 335 GPM — Q = 500 × √(45/100) = 500 × 0.671 = 335 GPM
    • D) 45 GPM

    Question 2 — Understand

    A process engineer asks why a Coriolis meter doesn't need a density correction for mass flow while an orifice plate does. What is the explanation?

    • A) A Coriolis meter measures the inertia of the flowing mass directly via the Coriolis effect — the phase shift between sensor signals is proportional to mass flow rate itself, regardless of fluid density or composition. An orifice plate measures velocity (via DP), so converting to mass flow requires multiplying by density, which must be measured or assumed.
    • B) Coriolis meters use a compensating temperature sensor
    • C) Orifice plates only work for gases
    • D) Coriolis meters are always more accurate

    Question 3 — Evaluate

    A specialty chemical plant doses three liquid reagents into a batch reactor. Each reagent has a different density that varies ±5% based on temperature and concentration during filling. The batch recipe requires ±0.1% mass dosing accuracy. Which meter type is required, and why?

    • A) Coriolis (Micro Motion) — because Coriolis measures mass flow directly, density variations have zero effect on accuracy. A DP or vortex meter measuring volume flow would accumulate mass error proportional to the density variation (±5% density = ±5% mass error), which is 50× the required accuracy.
    • B) Orifice plate with density correction
    • C) Vortex with temperature compensation
    • D) Magnetic meter

    Sales Implications

    Sales bridge — Flow measurement to Applied Control value

    1. Custody transfer is the highest-value flow conversation. Any plant buying or selling product by measurement has financial risk in meter accuracy. A 0.5% meter error on a pipeline carrying 10,000 BPD of crude at $70/barrel = $1.3M/year of uncertainty. Micro Motion ELITE at ±0.05% is the risk-reduction tool.
    2. Vortex meter replacement is a large installed base opportunity. Older vortex meters (pre-8800D) on steam have single-sensor designs susceptible to vibration. The 8800D multivariable with dual-sensor vibration rejection is a well-documented upgrade with documented ROI in steam loss reduction.
    3. Orifice plate replacement economics are straightforward. An orifice plate wears, plates get damaged, impulse lines plug. A Rosemount 3051SFC Annubar replacement eliminates the impulse lines (source of most DP flow problems), improves accuracy, and adds compensated mass flow — all at a cost that is typically recovered in one or two avoided maintenance events.
    4. Clamp-on ultrasonic as a diagnostic tool. Offer a portable clamp-on flow measurement service to verify existing meter accuracy. When the portable meter disagrees with the installed meter by more than 1–2%, the plant has a metering problem. Applied Control identifies the problem; the solution is a new Rosemount or Micro Motion meter.
