    Chapter 10: Positioners, Fieldbus & Digital Communication

    Learning Objectives

    • Remember: Name the three digital communication protocols used with Fisher FIELDVUE devices and state the signal standard for each
    • Understand: Explain how FOUNDATION Fieldbus differs from HART in its control architecture and why this matters for control loop response
    • Apply: Configure a DVC6200 for split-range control on a heating/cooling application using HART ranging commands
    • Analyze: Interpret a DVC6200 valve signature report and identify the key indicators of packing wear, spring degradation, and seat condition
    • Evaluate: Justify the choice between HART, FOUNDATION Fieldbus, and WirelessHART for three different instrumentation scenarios

    10.1 The Positioner Revisited

    Chapter 9 introduced the positioner as a position feedback controller. This chapter goes deeper into the digital communication layer that transforms positioners from dumb I/P converters into intelligent, diagnostic field devices integrated into the plant's asset management ecosystem.

    The shift from pneumatic-only positioners to digital valve controllers (DVCs) is arguably the most important change in control valve technology in the past 30 years. The DVC6200 is not just a more accurate positioner — it is a measurement instrument that reports valve health data continuously to the control room.

    10.2 HART Communication — Deep Dive

    HART (Highway Addressable Remote Transducer) was introduced in Chapter 3 for transmitters. For the DVC6200, HART enables a dramatically richer interaction:

    HART Variables for DVC6200

    Variable HART address Engineering units Diagnostic use
    Primary Variable (PV) PV % travel Actual valve position
    Secondary Variable (SV) SV mA Input current (commanded position)
    Tertiary Variable (TV) TV PSIG Actuator pressure
    Quaternary Variable (QV) QV PSIG Supply pressure

    The DCS can read all four variables simultaneously via HART multi-variable polling — providing a complete picture of valve state from the control room without a field visit.

    HART-Based DVC6200 Configuration

    All DVC6200 configuration is performed over HART — no DIP switches, no potentiometers. Key configurable parameters:

    Parameter Function Typical setting
    Travel range Sets 4 mA position and 20 mA position 0% and 100% travel
    Action Direct (4mA=closed, 20mA=open) or Reverse Depends on fail-safe design
    Gain (proportional) Position loop gain — affects response speed 2–4 typical; tune for stability
    Travel cutoff (low) Tight shutoff below this % — drives to hard seat 1–3%
    Friction compensation Adds impulse to overcome static friction Enable for high-packing friction
    Alert thresholds Travel deviation, friction limit, supply pressure low Set per application criticality

    Split-Range Configuration

    In heating/cooling control, a single controller output (4–20 mA) drives two valves — one for heating (steam), one for cooling (coolant). The positioners are ranged to respond to different portions of the signal:

    • Cooling valve: Ranged 4–12 mA (0–100% open) — fully open at 4 mA, closed at 12 mA
    • Heating valve: Ranged 12–20 mA (0–100% open) — closed at 12 mA, fully open at 20 mA

    At 12 mA, both valves are closed — the dead band. The DVC6200 split ranging is configured entirely in HART with no hardware modification.

    10.3 FOUNDATION Fieldbus

    FOUNDATION Fieldbus (FF) is a fully digital, two-wire bus protocol that replaces the 4–20 mA analog signal with an all-digital signal. Multiple devices share the same pair of wires; control can execute in the field device itself.

    How FF Differs from HART

    Characteristic HART FOUNDATION Fieldbus
    Signal 4–20 mA + digital overlay All-digital (31.25 kbps, H1)
    Devices per wire pair 1 (multidrop: up to 15, no analog) 2–32 devices
    Control execution DCS executes control Can execute in field device (function blocks)
    Update rate 250–500 ms (digital) 50 ms or faster
    Interoperability Device-type specific Standardized function blocks (AI, AO, PID)

    FF Architecture

    An FF segment consists of: - H1 segment wire pair at 31.25 kbps - Fieldbus power conditioner — provides segment power and isolates DCS - Up to 32 devices (practical limit: 8–16 for control loops with good response) - Link Active Scheduler (LAS) — manages segment timing; embedded in DCS interface card or in one smart field device

    The DVC6200 with FF option contains a PID function block — the entire control loop (measurement input, PID calculation, valve output) can execute within the field device at scan rates as fast as 50 ms, independent of DCS scan rate.

    When to Choose FF

    • New greenfield plant design where a full FF infrastructure is planned from the start
    • Applications requiring sub-100 ms control response independent of DCS scan rate
    • Projects where wire reduction economics are significant (1 wire pair vs. 12+ for 12 devices)
    • Emerson DeltaV-based DCS (DeltaV has the best FF integration of any DCS platform)

    When HART is Preferred

    • Brownfield retrofit (existing 4–20 mA infrastructure)
    • Plants with legacy DCS not supporting FF
    • Applications where the DCS executes all control and FF's field-execution capability is not needed
    • When device variety and procurement flexibility matter (larger HART device ecosystem)

    10.4 PROFIBUS PA

    PROFIBUS PA (Process Automation) is the IEC 61158 process fieldbus widely used in Europe and in plants with Siemens or ABB DCS systems. Signal standard: 31.25 kbps, two-wire bus, similar to FF H1 physically but with a different protocol layer.

    The DVC6200 is available with PROFIBUS PA option for plants operating Siemens PCS7 or ABB 800xA — ensuring Applied Control can serve customers regardless of their DCS platform.

    10.5 WirelessHART for Valves

    WirelessHART (Chapter 3) can be applied to positioners in monitoring-only scenarios. A wireless adapter (Emerson Wireless THUM) attached to the DVC6200 HART terminals transmits diagnostic data to a WirelessHART gateway without any wired connection.

    Applications: - Monitoring valve diagnostics in remote or hard-to-reach locations without running new HART wiring - Adding valve health visibility to legacy pneumatic-only valves (pair with a position transmitter) - Temporary diagnostic campaigns (mount adapters during a survey, remove after)

    Limitation: WirelessHART is monitoring-only — the 4–20 mA wired connection still controls the valve. You cannot operate a safety valve or control loop over WirelessHART.

    10.6 ValveLink — Valve Management Software

    Emerson ValveLink is the PC-based valve management application that provides the most complete interface to DVC6200 diagnostics:

    ValveLink Capabilities

    Feature Function
    Valve signature Graphical display of supply pressure, actuator pressure, and travel through full stroke
    Step response Tests valve response time to step changes in input — measures hysteresis and deadband
    Friction analysis Calculates running and static friction from signature data
    Performance diagnostics Automated analysis of signature vs. baseline — flags deviations
    Report generation PDF reports suitable for PSM documentation
    Device configuration Full DVC6200 parameter read/write over HART
    Offline configuration Build configuration without connecting to device; download when connected

    ValveLink runs on a PC connected to a HART modem (USB or serial). It can also operate through AMS Device Manager or DeltaV Live for plant-wide access without dedicated PC cabling.

    Interpreting a Valve Signature

    The valve signature is the graphical fingerprint of a control valve assembly's mechanical health:

    Supply pressure (PSIG)
        │
   ─────┼─────────────────────  (flat line = regulator OK)
        │
Actuator│    /─────────────────  (opening stroke)
pressure│   /
(PSIG)  │  /  hysteresis band
        │ /─────────────────────  (closing stroke)
        │/
        └────────────────────── Travel (%)
                 0%           100%

    Reading the signature: - Hysteresis band width → packing friction (wider = more friction = wear) - Slope of pressure vs. travel → spring rate (changed slope = spring degradation) - Pressure spike near 0% travel → seat load (excessive spike = over-seating force from oversized actuator) - Irregular steps or jumps → sticky packing or worn stem - Step change in pressure without travel change → diaphragm leak (pressure doesn't hold)

    10.7 AMS Device Manager Integration

    Emerson AMS Device Manager is the enterprise-level asset management platform that aggregates diagnostic data from all HART and FF field devices across the plant:

    Function Description
    Device health dashboard Color-coded status (OK / Advisory / Maintenance / Failed) for all devices
    Alert management Routes device alerts to maintenance work orders
    Historical trending Friction, travel deviation, supply pressure — trended over months/years
    Scheduled diagnostics Automatically runs valve signature tests on a schedule
    Calibration management Tracks calibration intervals; generates calibration records
    Device configuration history Records all configuration changes with timestamp and user

    The AMS-DVC6200 combination is Applied Control's most powerful reliability tool. A plant with 200 DVC6200-equipped valves in AMS has continuous health monitoring of every critical valve, with alerts surfacing in the control room before failures occur.

    Chapter Summary

    The DVC6200 FIELDVUE digital valve controller communicates over HART, FOUNDATION Fieldbus, PROFIBUS PA, or WirelessHART — each protocol suited to different plant architectures. HART is the dominant protocol for brownfield and mixed-fleet plants; FF enables field-level control execution and is optimal for greenfield DeltaV plants. The DVC6200's diagnostic value is realized through ValveLink and AMS Device Manager — both providing valve signature analysis, friction trending, and alert management that prevent failures. Valve signatures are the diagnostic fingerprint of every valve's mechanical health: packing friction shows in hysteresis band width, spring condition shows in slope, and seat condition shows in the force profile near closure. This diagnostic ecosystem is the foundation of Applied Control's predictive maintenance value proposition.

    • A) Position, temperature, flow, pressure

    • D) Position, set point, error, output

    Question 2 — Understand

    A DVC6200 valve signature shows the hysteresis band has widened from 8 PSIG to 22 PSIG over the past 18 months. What does this indicate, and what is the recommended action?

    • A) The spring has degraded — replace actuator spring
    • B) Packing friction has increased significantly — schedule packing replacement before the valve begins to show travel deviation and control loop oscillation
    • C) The actuator is undersized — replace with larger actuator
    • D) The supply pressure is too low — increase regulator setting

    Question 3 — Evaluate

    A refinery is designing a new crude unit with 150 control valves. The project engineer asks whether to use HART or FOUNDATION Fieldbus. The DCS is Emerson DeltaV. What is your recommendation?

    • A) HART — simpler wiring and more device options
    • B) FOUNDATION Fieldbus — DeltaV has the industry's best FF integration; FF reduces wiring cost (multiple devices per segment), enables faster control execution in critical loops, and future-proofs the plant for advanced diagnostics. For non-critical utilities, HART is acceptable as a fallback.
    • C) PROFIBUS — international standard
    • D) WirelessHART — eliminates all wiring
