    Chapter 18: Sales Engineering — Turning Expertise into Revenue

    Learning Objectives

    • Remember: Identify the five phases of the Applied Control sales process and the key stakeholders in each
    • Understand: Explain how technical depth translates to competitive differentiation and customer loyalty
    • Apply: Conduct a discovery conversation that surfaces unstated needs and positions the full Applied Control portfolio
    • Analyze: Build a total cost of ownership (TCO) analysis that quantifies the value of Fisher/Emerson vs. alternatives
    • Evaluate: Select the optimal Applied Control solution for a complex customer scenario and articulate its business case

    18.1 What Makes Applied Control Different

    Applied Control is not a distributor. It is an Emerson Impact Partner — one of a select tier of regional specialists who provide not just products but engineering expertise, field service, and lifecycle support. Understanding this distinction is the foundation of every sales conversation.

    The Applied Control value proposition:

    Competitor claims Applied Control delivers
    "We carry Fisher" We are Fisher experts — certified repair, sizing, configuration
    "We have the part" We have the engineering knowledge to ensure it's the right part
    "24-hour delivery" 24-hour delivery and the expert who knows if it will work
    "Competitive pricing" Lower lifetime cost through correct sizing and preventive diagnostics

    The five pillars of Applied Control differentiation:

    1. Fisher valve expertise — The deepest Fisher product knowledge in the region, from standard ED/EZ to severe service and specialty trim
    2. National Board Certified valve shop — Repair, remanufacture, and certify Fisher valves and pressure relief valves to as-new specification
    3. FIELDVUE diagnostics — Baseline, trend, and analyze every valve in the plant using DVC6200 and AMS Device Manager
    4. DeltaV system integration — Full Emerson DCS/SIS design, configuration, and commissioning capability
    5. Local presence, Emerson depth — Same-day field service backed by Emerson's global supply chain, engineering resources, and 24/7 support line

    18.2 The Five Sales Phases

    Phase 1: Territory Mapping

    Before calling on accounts, map the territory:

    • Who are the large industrial users? (refinery, chemical plant, power generation, water treatment, food/beverage, pharmaceutical)
    • What is the installed base? Fisher valves already in the plant are immediate maintenance, upgrade, and diagnostic opportunities
    • Who are the key decision-makers? Plant manager (P&L), maintenance supervisor (reliability), process engineer (performance), safety manager (SIS compliance), purchasing (price)

    Applied Control CRM: Every customer site, every valve population, every planned outage and turnaround should be in the CRM. The sales process begins months before the customer calls.

    Phase 2: Discovery

    Discovery is the most important phase. The goal is to understand problems the customer hasn't stated yet.

    The five discovery questions (for any plant visit):

    1. "How are your control loops performing — any hunting, oscillation, or offset?" → Opens positioner upgrade, packing replacement, valve diagnostic conversation

    2. "When did you last have an unplanned shutdown related to a valve or instrument?" → Opens reliability, DVC6200 diagnostics, valve shop discussion

    3. "Are you planning any turnarounds or outages in the next 6–18 months?" → Opens proactive repair, spare parts, remanufacture conversation

    4. "What standards are you under for your safety instrumented functions?" → Opens DeltaV SIS, IEC 61511, partial stroke testing conversation

    5. "What does your vendor relationship look like for DCS support?" → Opens DeltaV migration or support contract conversation

    Listen for pain, not for product fit. Don't pitch until you understand the problem.

    Phase 3: Technical Qualification

    Once a problem is identified, apply technical depth to define the solution:

    • Get the P&ID, data sheet, or service record
    • Size the valve or positioner correctly (don't guess)
    • Calculate cavitation index if liquid service with significant ΔP
    • Calculate noise level for gas/steam service
    • Determine actuator thrust from stem force requirements
    • Specify the correct Fisher model, body size, trim, leakage class, actuator, positioner

    This is where subject matter expertise closes the deal. A competitor who quotes from a catalog cannot match a proposal that includes sizing calculations, cavitation analysis, and a documented justification for each specification choice.

    Phase 4: Proposal and Business Case

    Every significant proposal should include:

    1. Technical specification — What we recommend and why (from the sizing/analysis)
    2. Business case / TCO — Why it costs less over the life of the asset (see Section 18.4)
    3. Applied Control differentiators — Specifically why Applied Control over a generic distributor
    4. Risk statement — What happens if the alternative (cheaper) solution is chosen

    Phase 5: Lifecycle Engagement

    The sale doesn't end at the PO. The relationship deepens after installation:

    • Baseline valve signature after installation (DVC6200)
    • Annual diagnostic review — friction trends, alert history, recommendations
    • Turnaround support — Applied Control field service team on-site during outages
    • AMS Device Manager report — Semi-annual or annual valve population health report

    A customer who receives a diagnostic report every year sees Applied Control as a partner, not a vendor. This creates loyalty that no competitor can easily displace with a 10% price reduction.

    18.3 Stakeholder Mapping

    Different stakeholders have different motivations. Tailor the conversation:

    Stakeholder Primary concern What Applied Control says
    Plant Manager Uptime, production, cost "We reduced unplanned valve downtime by X% for [similar plant]. Here's how."
    Maintenance Supervisor Reliability, predictability "DVC6200 diagnostics tell you a valve is failing 3 months before it fails."
    Process Engineer Performance, loop control "Fisher ED's 50:1 rangeability gives you tighter control at turndown. Let me size it."
    Safety Manager Compliance, IEC 61511 "PST with DVC6200 documents your proof tests for SIL 2 compliance automatically."
    Purchasing Price, lead time "Our TCO analysis shows Fisher costs 23% less over 10 years vs. generic. Here's the math."
    Instrument Technician Ease of use, diagnostics "The 375 Field Communicator talks to DVC6200 in 30 seconds. No proprietary software on a laptop."

    18.4 Total Cost of Ownership — The TCO Argument

    TCO analysis is the most powerful tool for justifying Fisher vs. generic alternatives. Build it with real numbers whenever possible.

    TCO elements for a control valve:

    Cost category Generic alternative Fisher ED + DVC6200
    Purchase price Lower Higher (+15–30%)
    Installation Similar Similar
    Diagnostics None (additional analyzer needed) Included in DVC6200
    Maintenance frequency Reactive (avg. 2.5 yr cycle) Predictive (avg. 4.5 yr cycle based on signature)
    Unplanned downtime cost 1 event/5 yr avg. 0.2 events/5 yr avg. with DVC6200
    Trim replacement (cavitation) Every 12–18 months (unprotected) Every 5+ years (Cavitrol III protected)
    Repair labor Internal + contractor Applied Control valve shop (faster turnaround)
    Energy loss (leakage) Class II (0.5% Cv) Class IV (0.01% Cv) — 50× less leakage

    Example TCO calculation:

    A chemical plant has 50 control valves. Average unplanned shutdown cost: $120,000. Probability of valve-caused shutdown without diagnostics: 1 per year. With DVC6200 diagnostics: 0.2 per year.

    Annual risk reduction = (1.0 - 0.2) × $120,000 = $96,000/year
DVC6200 upgrade cost (50 valves) = $125,000
Payback period = $125,000 / $96,000 = 1.3 years
10-year NPV of diagnostic investment: > $700,000

    Present this math, not a brochure.

    • DeltaV expertise: System integration capability goes beyond most distributors
    • Local field service team: Rapid response for critical failures
    • Application engineering depth: In-house sizing, noise calculation, severe service specification

    18.6 Industry-Specific Talking Points

    Refining and Petrochemical

    • Key pain: High-temperature, high-pressure service destroys valve trim; unplanned downtime is catastrophic
    • Key products: Fisher HP series (Class 600–4500), Cavitrol III, WhisperFlo, DVC6200 with partial stroke testing
    • Key message: "Fisher trim in refinery service outlasts generic by 2–3 years. We can document this from your historian."
    • Entry point: Turnaround planning 12–18 months ahead

    Power Generation

    • Key pain: Steam conditioning, feedwater control, boiler drum level — all high-stakes; SIS requirements
    • Key products: Fisher EHD, HP, steam conditioning valve, DeltaV SIS, Rosemount 3051 DP transmitters
    • Key message: "Every missed generation MW is revenue lost. Our valve diagnostics prevent the trips that cost you dispatch."
    • Entry point: Control system obsolescence (legacy DCS end-of-life)

    Chemical / Specialty Chemical

    • Key pain: Corrosive and reactive fluids; strict product quality; batch control precision
    • Key products: Fisher D3 lined valve, PTFE/Hastelloy trim options, DeltaV batch control, Micro Motion Coriolis
    • Key message: "Tight shutoff and precise throttling protect product yield and prevent batch rejects."
    • Entry point: Material compatibility review

    Water and Wastewater

    • Key pain: Large flow rates, low cost pressure, reliability across remote sites
    • Key products: Fisher V150/V200 Vee-Ball, butterfly valves, Rosemount pressure and level transmitters
    • Key message: "The V-notch Vee-Ball handles solids in produced water — no plugging, consistent characterization."
    • Entry point: Aging infrastructure replacement

    Food and Beverage / Life Sciences

    • Key pain: Sanitary requirements, validation, cGMP compliance, product purity
    • Key products: Fisher FIELDVUE with 21 CFR Part 11 audit trail, sanitary valve trims, Rosemount sanitary transmitters
    • Key message: "Full electronic audit trail on valve position and diagnostics supports FDA/cGMP validation documentation."
    • Entry point: FDA audit or system validation project

    18.7 Objection Handling

    "Your price is too high"

    "Compared to what? Let me walk you through the TCO. Over 10 years, the Fisher ED with DVC6200 costs significantly less than the alternative when you include maintenance cycles, unplanned downtime, and repair costs. Should we look at the last three years of your maintenance records and build the actual numbers?"

    "We already have a vendor relationship with [competitor]"

    "That's great — it means you know what reliable support looks like. The question is whether they provide the same level of engineering support, local repair capability, and diagnostic program we do. Would you be open to running a side-by-side comparison on your next valve purchase?"

    "We just buy Fisher direct"

    "You can buy the product direct — what you can't buy direct is the engineering services, the local repair shop, the diagnostic program, and the field service team. Those are Applied Control, not Fisher."

    "We don't have budget this quarter"

    "Understood — let's talk about what's going on in the plant that I can help with now at no cost: I can run a diagnostic on your DVC6200-equipped valves from the AMS system in your control room in about an hour. That report might surface something that justifies the conversation with your plant manager about budget."

    18.8 The Applied Control Conversation Starters

    For every plant visit, have at least two of these ready:

    1. The diagnostic offer: "Can I pull your DVC6200 valve data while I'm here? I'll generate a friction trend report — five minutes from your AMS system."
    2. The sizing challenge: "What's your worst-performing loop? Send me the data sheet and I'll tell you in 24 hours whether the valve is sized correctly."
    3. The turnaround question: "When is your next planned outage? I want to make sure we're in your planning process early enough to quote repair or replacement in time."
    4. The safety system question: "Who manages your SIS proof test schedule? I want to talk to them about partial stroke testing with your existing DVC6200s — it might eliminate manual pull-and-test procedures."
    5. The obsolescence conversation: "What DCS are you running? If it's more than 15 years old, we should talk about your migration options — DeltaV can typically be phased in without taking down the plant."

    Chapter Summary

    Sales engineering at Applied Control is the application of technical expertise in service of customer outcomes. The process flows through five phases: territory mapping, discovery, technical qualification, proposal, and lifecycle engagement. Effective discovery surfaces unstated problems before competitors know they exist. Technical depth — sizing, cavitation analysis, noise calculation, diagnostic interpretation — is the primary competitive differentiation. TCO analysis quantifies what technical depth is worth in dollars. Stakeholder mapping ensures the right message reaches the right person. Industry-specific knowledge opens conversations and positions Applied Control as a specialist, not a catalog distributor.

    The sales engineer who can solve the customer's problem before the customer knows they have it is the sales engineer who will never lose a deal on price.

    Build the 5-year TCO comparison. What is the payback period?
