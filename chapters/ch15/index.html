    Chapter 15: Standards, Codes & Compliance

    Learning Objectives

    • Remember: Match the following standards to their governing bodies and scope: IEC 61511, API 526, ASME B16.34, IEC 60534, ANSI/ISA-75.01
    • Understand: Explain the difference between a code (mandatory) and a standard (voluntary) and give examples of each in process control
    • Apply: Determine the minimum SIL rating required for a safety function given a target risk reduction factor of 500
    • Analyze: Identify which clauses of IEC 61511 apply to the proof testing of a SIL 2 shutdown valve in a natural gas processing plant
    • Evaluate: Justify the process safety documentation requirements that OSHA PSM (29 CFR 1910.119) imposes on a refinery and explain Applied Control's role in meeting each requirement

    15.1 Why Standards Matter in Valve Automation

    Standards and codes govern virtually every aspect of the products Applied Control sells and services. A sales engineer who can reference the relevant standard is demonstrating expertise and credibility — a sales engineer who doesn't know which standard applies is demonstrating the opposite.

    Three categories:

    Category Definition Examples
    Codes Mandatory requirements enforced by law or regulation ASME Boiler and Pressure Vessel Code (BPVC), OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119
    Standards Consensus documents — mandatory only when referenced in a contract or code IEC 61511, API 520, IEC 60534
    Guidelines Recommended practices — informational only API 521, ISA-TR96.05

    15.2 Valve Body and Pressure Standards

    ASME B16.34 — Valves — Flanged, Threaded, and Welding End

    ASME B16.34 is the governing standard for pressure-temperature ratings of industrial valves. It defines: - Pressure classes: Class 150, 300, 600, 900, 1500, 2500 (ANSI classes for flanged end valves) - Pressure-temperature rating tables for each material group and class - Testing requirements: Shell test, seat test, and backseat test procedures - Materials: Acceptable materials for each service temperature range

    Reading a valve's pressure class:

    A Fisher ED control valve rated Class 300 in WCC (carbon steel) body has: - Maximum allowable pressure at 100°F: 720 PSIG - Maximum allowable pressure at 400°F: 655 PSIG
    - Maximum allowable pressure at 750°F: 410 PSIG

    The pressure rating decreases with temperature — this relationship is defined in ASME B16.34 material group tables.

    ANSI/FCI 70-2 — Valve Leakage Classification

    ANSI/FCI 70-2 (now incorporated in IEC 60534-4) defines the standard leakage classes:

    Class Maximum leakage Test method
    I No test required —
    II 0.5% of rated Cv Water at 50–125 PSID
    III 0.1% of rated Cv Water at 50–125 PSID
    IV 0.01% of rated Cv Water at 50–125 PSID
    V 5×10⁻⁴ mL/min × PSID × in seat dia Water at max operating ΔP
    VI Bubble-tight (table by seat size) Air/N₂ at 50 PSIG or max ΔP

    Fisher standard offerings by trim design: - Standard metal seat: Class IV - Cavitrol/WhisperFlo trim: Class IV - Soft seat (PTFE/elastomer): Class VI - Class V: Available on request for specific Fisher models

    15.3 IEC 60534 — Industrial-Process Control Valves

    IEC 60534 is the international control valve standard. Its parts cover:

    Part Subject
    IEC 60534-1 Terminology and general considerations
    IEC 60534-2-1 Flow capacity: sizing equations for incompressible and compressible fluids
    IEC 60534-2-3 Test procedures to verify flow capacity
    IEC 60534-3-1 Dimensions of face-to-face and end-to-end for flanged valves
    IEC 60534-4 Leakage classification (equivalent to ANSI/FCI 70-2)
    IEC 60534-8-3 Aerodynamic noise calculation
    IEC 60534-8-4 Hydrodynamic noise calculation

    The sizing equations in IEC 60534-2-1 are the foundation of all Cv-based valve sizing (Chapters 5 and beyond). Fisher's ValveLink sizing software implements these equations.

    15.4 API Standards for Pressure Relief

    Standard Scope
    API 520 Part I Sizing of pressure relieving devices
    API 520 Part II Installation of pressure relieving devices
    API 521 Guide for pressure-relieving and depressuring systems
    API 526 Standard flanged steel pressure relief valves (orifice sizes, dimensions)
    API 527 Seat tightness of pressure relief valves
    API 576 Inspection of pressure-relieving devices

    API 576 is the most operationally relevant for Applied Control's valve shop: - Defines inspection and testing intervals for PRVs - Requires documentation of as-found and as-left test conditions - Specifies acceptable as-found tolerances (±5% of set pressure for most valves) - Guides repair scope (when to recondition vs. replace)

    15.5 IEC 61511 — Functional Safety for SIS

    IEC 61511 (equivalent to ISA-84 in the US) is the process sector application standard for Safety Instrumented Systems. It defines requirements for the entire safety lifecycle:

    Safety Lifecycle

    1. Hazard and risk analysis — identify process hazards (HAZOP, LOPA)
    2. Safety requirements specification — define SIL requirements for each SIF
    3. SIS design — select sensors, logic solver, final elements to achieve the SIL
    4. SIS installation and commissioning — install per design; document as-built
    5. SIS validation — demonstrate SIL achievement through testing
    6. SIS operation and maintenance — proof testing, management of change
    7. SIS modification — change control; re-validation
    8. SIS decommissioning — safe disposal of safety functions

    Layer of Protection Analysis (LOPA)

    LOPA is the quantitative risk analysis method most commonly used to determine SIL requirements:

    1. Identify the initiating event (e.g., operator error, control valve failure)
    2. Identify the consequence (explosion, toxic release, equipment damage)
    3. Assess the consequence severity and frequency of the initiating event
    4. Identify existing independent protection layers (IPLs) that reduce risk
    5. Calculate the residual risk after IPLs
    6. If residual risk exceeds tolerable risk → SIS required; SIL determined by required risk reduction

    Required SIL example:

    Initiating event frequency: 0.1/year. Consequence: catastrophic (requires 10⁻⁶/year tolerable risk). Existing IPLs reduce by 10⁻³. Residual risk without SIS: 10⁻⁴/year. Target: 10⁻⁶/year. Required risk reduction by SIS: 100 → SIL 2 (10⁻² to 10⁻³ PFD range).

    Proof Testing Requirements

    Proof test = a functional test that confirms the SIS will respond correctly to a demand. IEC 61511 requires proof testing at an interval determined by the SIL target:

    SIL Required test interval (typical) Test coverage
    SIL 1 Every 1–5 years >60% dangerous failures detected
    SIL 2 Every 1–3 years >90% dangerous failures detected
    SIL 3 Every 6 months–1 year >99% dangerous failures detected

    PST contribution to proof test coverage: Partial stroke testing of SIS valves detects mechanical failures in the valve assembly (stuck stem, failed actuator). A PST with DVC6200 provides approximately 50–60% proof test coverage for the final element, reducing the frequency of full proof tests (complete valve stroke to closed, which may require process shutdown).

    15.6 OSHA Process Safety Management (PSM)

    OSHA 29 CFR 1910.119 (PSM) is US federal law applicable to facilities that use highly hazardous chemicals above threshold quantities. PSM has 14 elements; several directly involve Applied Control:

    Relevant PSM Elements

    Process Safety Information (PSI): PSM requires complete documentation of the process: P&IDs, equipment data, valve data sheets, safety system descriptions. Applied Control's role: maintain valve data sheets with correct specifications; provide DVC6200 configuration records.

    Mechanical Integrity (MI): PSM requires documented inspection, testing, and maintenance of equipment in covered processes. For pressure relief valves: test intervals, as-found data, repair records, and certification must be maintained. Applied Control's valve shop provides the test certificates that satisfy PSM MI documentation for PRVs.

    Management of Change (MOC): Any change to process equipment (including valves) in a PSM-covered facility requires formal change review before implementation. Applied Control should notify customers when recommending valve trim changes or positioner configuration changes — these are MOC-triggering changes in PSM plants.

    Pre-Startup Safety Review (PSSR): New or modified equipment requires a safety review before startup. Documentation of valve configuration, DVC6200 baseline signature, and PRV certification is part of the PSSR package.

    15.7 ATEX and Hazardous Area Classification

    Process plants have hazardous areas — zones where flammable gases, vapors, or dusts may be present and where ignition sources (sparks, hot surfaces) could cause explosion. Electrical equipment in these zones must be certified for the hazardous area classification.

    NEC (US) classification system:

    Class Hazard
    Class I Flammable gases or vapors
    Class II Combustible dusts
    Class III Ignitable fibers

    Division system:

    Division Hazard present
    Division 1 Under normal operating conditions
    Division 2 Only under abnormal conditions (failure, maintenance)

    IEC/ATEX (International) zone system:

    Zone Gas/vapor
    Zone 0 Continuously present
    Zone 1 Frequently present
    Zone 2 Occasionally present

    Fisher DVC6200 certifications: - FM Explosionproof: Class I, Division 1 and 2 - ATEX II 2G (Zone 1): Ex d IIC T6 - CSA Explosionproof and Intrinsically Safe

    When specifying a DVC6200 for a hazardous area, confirm the area classification and select the matching certification mark. The wrong certification can create an OSHA citation — the right certification protects the plant.

    Chapter Summary

    Standards govern every product Applied Control sells and every service it provides. ASME B16.34 defines pressure class and material ratings for valve bodies. ANSI/FCI 70-2 defines leakage class. IEC 60534 governs control valve sizing. API 520/526 govern pressure relief valve sizing and dimensions. IEC 61511 is the safety lifecycle standard for SIS — driving SIL requirements, logic solver selection, and proof test frequency. OSHA PSM creates the mechanical integrity and documentation requirements that make Applied Control's PRV testing and DVC6200 configuration services legally valuable, not just technically useful. Hazardous area classification determines which product certification marks are required — an area where Applied Control expertise protects customers from compliance risk.

    Sales Implications

    Sales bridge — Standards and compliance to Applied Control value

    1. PSM mechanical integrity is a legal requirement, not a preference. When a plant's PRV testing program is not to the standard required by OSHA PSM, the plant faces regulatory risk. Applied Control's National Board certified shop provides the legally defensible test record. Frame PRV services as compliance, not just maintenance.
    2. IEC 61511 drives hardware selection. When the SIS team is designing a new safety function and selecting the final element, Applied Control's knowledge of DVC6200 SIL certification, PST coverage, and HART diagnostic integration is critical. Get in the conversation early — before the SIS design is fixed.
    3. MOC is a sales trigger. Every Management of Change in a PSM plant is reviewed by the process safety team. When Applied Control recommends a valve upgrade, offer to support the MOC documentation — valve data sheet, pressure class verification, DVC6200 ATEX certification documentation. This support is a service competitors don't provide.
    4. ATEX/FM certification prevents costly mistakes. A customer who specifies the wrong hazardous area protection method faces OSHA inspection risk. Applied Control verifying that every DVC6200 shipped has the correct FM or ATEX mark for the area classification is a genuine value-add that prevents regulatory problems.
