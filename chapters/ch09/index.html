    Chapter 9: Actuators and Positioners — Moving the Valve

    Learning Objectives

    • Remember: Name the three main actuator types and state which is most common in process control
    • Understand: Explain how a spring-diaphragm actuator produces force and why fail-safe action matters
    • Apply: Select the correct actuator size for a given valve thrust requirement
    • Analyze: Interpret a FIELDVUE DVC6200 valve signature and identify what it reveals about valve health
    • Evaluate: Justify the premium cost of a DVC6200 vs. a conventional positioner using diagnostic and loop performance benefits

    9.1 The Actuator's Job

    The actuator is the muscle of the control valve assembly. It converts the controller's output signal (typically 4–20 mA or pneumatic 3–15 PSIG) into mechanical force and motion — moving the plug, ball, or disk to the position commanded by the controller.

    Three requirements an actuator must meet: 1. Sufficient force to stroke the valve against process differential pressure, packing friction, and spring loads 2. Sufficient speed to respond to the process loop's demand 3. Correct fail-safe action — the position the valve moves to on loss of signal or supply

    9.2 Pneumatic Actuators — The Industry Standard

    Pneumatic actuators dominate process control because: - Instrument air is always available in process plants - Spring-diaphragm design is simple, reliable, and self-contained - Inherently fail-safe (spring return on signal loss) - No electrical hazardous area classification required - Force output scales simply with diaphragm area × pressure

    Fisher 657 Diaphragm Actuator (Spring-to-Open)

    The Fisher 657 is the world's most widely deployed pneumatic actuator. It is a spring-to-open, signal-to-close design:

    Feature Specification
    Action Diaphragm case pressurizes → stem moves down (push-down-to-close)
    Fail action Air fail: spring pushes stem up → valve opens on air failure
    Spring range 3–15 PSIG, 6–30 PSIG, or custom
    Sizes 30, 34, 40, 46, 60, 70, 80, 100 in² effective diaphragm area
    Stroke Up to 4" standard; up to 6" extended
    Temperature –40°F to +150°F (actuator air space)

    Actuator sizing — thrust calculation:

    $$F_{required} = F_{packing} + F_{seat} + F_{stem} + F_{unbalance}$$

    Where: - F_packing = packing friction (empirical; depends on packing style and stem diameter) - F_seat = seating load required for the specified leakage class (Class V/VI > Class IV) - F_stem = stem area × ΔP (for balanced vs. unbalanced trim) - F_unbalance = differential pressure force across trim if unbalanced design

    Fisher's actuator sizing software (ValveLink) performs this calculation and selects the smallest actuator size that meets force requirements with margin.

    Fisher 667 Diaphragm Actuator (Spring-to-Close)

    The Fisher 667 is the spring-to-close, signal-to-open variant:

    • Air pressure pushes stem up → valve opens
    • Air failure → spring pushes stem down → valve closes
    • Correct for fail-closed applications: emergency shutoff, fuel valves, high-pressure gas letdown

    Fail-safe selection guide:

    Service Preferred fail position Actuator type
    Fuel or combustion air Fail-closed Fisher 667 (spring-to-close)
    Coolant/cooling water Fail-open Fisher 657 (spring-to-open)
    Level control (overflow risk) Fail-closed Fisher 667
    Steam to reboiler Fail-closed Fisher 667
    Safety system isolation Depends on SIL analysis Per SIL calculation

    9.3 Electric Actuators

    Electric actuators use an electric motor driving through a gearbox to position the valve. They are preferred when: - No instrument air is available (remote sites, offshore platforms, buildings) - Very precise position control is needed (pharmaceutical batch processes) - High thrust is required without a large pneumatic supply (large gate/globe valves)

    Fisher EIM electric actuators cover torques up to 150,000 in-lb for large quarter-turn valves. Features include: - Integral position feedback (potentiometer or encoder) - Limit switches for open/closed position - Handwheel for manual operation during maintenance - HART or FOUNDATION Fieldbus communication options

    Trade-offs vs. pneumatic: - Slower stroke speed (seconds to minutes vs. sub-second for pneumatic) - More complex maintenance (motor, gearbox, controls) - Higher purchase cost for comparable thrust - Excellent for slow processes (temperature, large vessel level)

    9.4 Hydraulic Actuators

    Hydraulic actuators use pressurized oil (typically 1,500–3,000 PSIG) to produce very high force in a compact package. Used for: - Extremely large valves (choke valves, large gate valves, pipeline mainline isolation) - High-cycle rate applications (compressor anti-surge) - Remote deep-sea or high-temperature installations

    Fisher YD series hydraulic actuators provide stroke forces up to 100,000 lbf for large subsea valves. Not common in general process plant service.

    9.5 Positioners — Closing the Position Loop

    A positioner is a local feedback controller that compares the commanded position (from the DCS, 4–20 mA) with the actual position (from the stem position feedback) and modulates the actuating pressure to eliminate any position error.

    Without a positioner: The 4–20 mA signal directly loads a current-to-pneumatic (I/P) converter, which sets actuator pressure. Packing friction, supply pressure variations, and stem force changes cause position errors.

    With a positioner: The positioner continuously corrects for all of these — it knows where the valve actually is and drives the actuator until the position matches the command.

    Why Positioners Are Now Standard

    On legacy systems, positioners were sometimes omitted to save cost. Today they are standard because:

    1. Tighter control: Position error < 0.5% of span vs. 5–10% without positioner
    2. Diagnostics: Modern positioners (DVC6200) report position, friction, signature, and alert on deviation — impossible without feedback
    3. Split-range control: Two valves controlled on different parts of the 4–20 mA signal requires positioners
    4. HART communication: Enables remote access to valve health data from the DCS workstation

    The DVC6200 is the flagship positioner from Fisher — the most sophisticated, most widely deployed intelligent positioner in the industry.

    Feedback Hall-effect travel sensor (contactless, non-wearing)

    Diagnostics Full valve signature, friction trend, travel deviation, alert history
    Mounting Direct-mount to Fisher actuators; universal for third-party

    DVC6200 Diagnostic Capabilities

    This is what separates the DVC6200 from any conventional positioner:

    1. Valve Signature (Stroke Test) The DVC6200 can perform an automated stroke test while the valve is in service (for non-critical loops) or offline. It records: - Supply pressure - Actuator pressure - Valve travel ...at every point through the full stroke. The resulting signature reveals:

    • Packing friction: Width of the hysteresis loop in the signature
    • Seat condition: Abnormal force signature near seating
    • Actuator spring rate: Slope of the signature — detects spring failure
    • Diaphragm leakage: Pressure hold test identifies air leaks

    2. Travel Deviation Alert If the actual valve position deviates from commanded position by more than a set threshold, the DVC6200 raises a Hart alert visible in the DCS or AMS Device Manager. This catches: - Packing sticking - Plug-seat contact preventing full close - Actuator pressure loss

    3. Friction Trending The DVC6200 tracks friction over time. A rising friction trend indicates packing wear before it causes control problems. Schedule the maintenance before the failure — not after.

    4. Partial Stroke Testing (PST) for SIS For safety-instrumented system valves, the DVC6200 can perform a partial stroke test — moving the valve 10–15% from the fully open position to prove mechanical integrity without disrupting process flow. Required by IEC 61511 for SIL 2 and SIL 3 applications to achieve proof test coverage targets.

    Accessing DVC6200 diagnostics

    DVC6200 diagnostics are accessed through: - HART handheld (375/475 Field Communicator): For field technicians during commissioning - Emerson AMS Device Manager: Plant-wide asset management; historical trending; alert management - Emerson DeltaV with DVC6200 integration: Real-time HART reads into the DCS historian; alerts appear on operator displays - ValveLink software: Dedicated valve management application; generates PDF reports for compliance documentation

    9.7 DVC2000 — Entry-Level Smart Positioner

    For less critical service or tighter budgets, the Fisher DVC2000 provides HART communication and basic diagnostics at lower cost:

    • Integral to actuator mounting (direct mount only)
    • HART communication for remote position readout
    • Basic friction and travel deviation alerts
    • No full valve signature capability
    • Appropriate for general process service where signature analysis is not required

    9.8 Volume Boosters and Accessories

    For fast-stroking applications (anti-surge, emergency shutdown), the positioner's output may not drive actuator pressure fast enough. Volume boosters solve this:

    • A volume booster amplifies the positioner's pilot signal with a high-flow relay
    • The actuator fills and exhausts at high flow rates (fast stroke)
    • The positioner controls precisely, the booster provides the flow capacity
    • Fisher 627 Series volume boosters: flow capacity up to 300 SCFM at 100 PSIG

    Accessory ecosystem:

    Accessory Function
    Volume booster Fast stroke speed
    Trip valve Pneumatic on/off override (ESD)
    Solenoid valve Electrical on/off isolation of air supply
    Supply regulator Filters and regulates instrument air to clean, constant pressure
    Lock-up valve Locks valve position on air supply failure (preserves last position)
    Limit switches Position feedback to DCS as discrete signal
    Position transmitter 4–20 mA position feedback to DCS

    Chapter Summary

    Actuators are the muscle of the control valve assembly. Fisher 657 (spring-to-open) and 667 (spring-to-close) diaphragm actuators cover the vast majority of process control applications; fail-safe action is a critical selection criterion. Positioners close the position feedback loop, eliminating errors from packing friction and supply pressure variation. The Fisher DVC6200 FIELDVUE positioner is the industry benchmark — not just for position control precision, but for its diagnostic capabilities: valve signature, friction trending, travel deviation alerts, and partial stroke testing. These diagnostics reduce unplanned downtime, justify maintenance scheduling, and enable SIS proof testing. The DVC6200 is Applied Control's strongest single product for proving value to a plant's maintenance and reliability organization.

    What is the fail-safe action of a Fisher 657 spring-diaphragm actuator?

    • A) Fail-closed (spring drives stem down on air failure)
    • B) Fail-open (spring drives stem up on air failure — with push-down-to-close trim)
    • C) Lock in last position
    • D) Depends on supply pressure

    Question 2 — Understand

    A control valve's hysteresis is 8% of span (very high). Without a positioner, what does this mean for loop control?

    • A) The loop will control tightly — the DCS will compensate
    • B) The valve will stick and jump — small controller output changes produce no movement, then a large position jump; the loop will hunt and oscillate
    • C) The loop gain will be reduced, making the loop sluggish but stable
    • D) The positioner senses this automatically and corrects it

    A plant maintenance manager says: "We can't justify the DVC6200 premium over a basic positioner — the valve works fine." What is the strongest single response?

    • A) The DVC6200 has better position accuracy

    • C) "The DVC6200 can detect a valve that is 'working fine' but slowly building friction — before it fails. One avoided unplanned shutdown typically pays for the entire positioner budget across a year. Can you show me the last time this valve was pulled and inspected?"
    • D) It meets the latest HART revision requirements

    Sales Implications

    Sales bridge — Actuators and positioners to plant value

    1. The positioner is the data source. Every DVC6200 is a sensor that reports valve health. Position it not as a "box on the actuator" but as the plant's window into its most critical final elements.
    2. Lead with reliability, not features. The maintenance manager doesn't care about HART Revision 7. They care about: How many unplanned shutdowns did we have last year, and how many were valve-related? The DVC6200 answers that question before the next shutdown.
    3. The upgrade conversation is easy. A plant with old pneumatic-only positioners has zero diagnostic visibility into ~50% of its valves. A DVC6200 retrofit requires no new wiring (4-20 mA HART rides on the same pair) and takes 2 hours per valve. ROI is typically less than one avoided maintenance call.
    4. Partial stroke testing is a regulatory driver. For any SIL 2+ safety valve, IEC 61511 requires documented proof tests. PST with the DVC6200 is the lowest-disruption method. Cite the standard when talking to the safety engineer — it closes.
    5. Applied Control's valve shop + DVC6200 = closed loop. We can install the DVC6200, capture a baseline signature, store it in AMS, and compare it 12 months later. If friction doubled, we schedule a shop rebuild. The customer never has a surprise failure.
