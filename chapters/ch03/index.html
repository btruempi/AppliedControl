    Chapter 3: Instrumentation — Sensing the Process

    Learning Objectives

    • Remember: Name the four process variables most commonly measured in industrial control and identify the standard signal range for process instruments
    • Understand: Explain how a differential pressure transmitter converts a physical pressure difference into a 4–20 mA signal, and why the 4 mA live-zero matters
    • Apply: Select the correct Rosemount transmitter family for a given measurement application (pressure, temperature, flow, level)
    • Analyze: Interpret a HART device descriptor and identify which process variables, diagnostic parameters, and alerts are available remotely
    • Evaluate: Justify the selection of WirelessHART vs. wired HART for a remote measurement point, weighing cost, accuracy, and power constraints

    3.1 The Four Process Variables

    Every industrial control loop begins with measurement. The four process variables that dominate process control instrumentation are:

    Variable Symbol Common Units Typical Sensor
    Pressure P PSIG, bar, kPa Diaphragm + capacitance cell
    Temperature T °F, °C RTD, thermocouple
    Flow F GPM, SCFM, kg/hr DP, vortex, Coriolis, magnetic
    Level L inches, %, ft DP, radar, guided wave radar

    A fifth variable — composition/analysis (pH, conductivity, dissolved oxygen, gas concentration) — is increasingly measured with online analyzers but follows the same signal and wiring conventions.

    3.2 The 4–20 mA Current Loop — The Universal Language

    The 4–20 mA current loop is the dominant signal standard in process instrumentation. A transmitter converts its measured variable into a proportional DC current:

    $$I = 4 + \frac{(PV - PV_{min})}{(PV_{max} - PV_{min})} \times 16 \text{ mA}$$

    Signal (mA) Meaning
    4 mA Zero / minimum range (live zero)
    12 mA 50% of range
    20 mA Full scale / maximum range
    < 3.6 mA Instrument fault / broken wire
    > 21 mA Over-range / saturated

    Why 4 mA and not 0 mA? The live zero is the key innovation. A broken wire reads 0 mA — immediately distinguishable from a real process zero at 4 mA. The loop current also powers the transmitter (two-wire transmitters draw their operating power from the loop current itself).

    Two-Wire vs. Four-Wire Transmitters

    Type Wiring Power source Use case
    Two-wire Signal+ / Signal− Loop-powered (DCS supplies 24 VDC) Vast majority of field instruments
    Four-wire Signal pair + separate power pair External 24 VDC or 120/240 VAC High-power analyzers, some flow meters

    3.3 HART Protocol — Digital on Analog

    HART (Highway Addressable Remote Transducer) superimposes a digital signal on the 4–20 mA current loop using Frequency Shift Keying (FSK):

    • 1200 Hz = digital "1"
    • 2200 Hz = digital "0"
    • The FSK signal averages to zero — it does not disturb the 4–20 mA measurement

    What HART enables:

    Capability Benefit
    Remote ranging (LRV/URV) Calibrate span without visiting the field
    Device identification Tag, serial number, model via DCS or handheld
    Secondary variables Flow transmitter can report temperature, density, totalizer simultaneously
    Diagnostic alerts Sensor drift, plugged impulse line, process noise level
    Loop current override Force output to specific mA for loop testing

    HART 5 vs. HART 7: HART 7 (current standard) adds WirelessHART support, enhanced device descriptions, and richer diagnostic data. Rosemount and Emerson products ship with HART 7 by default; all HART 7 devices communicate with HART 5 masters.

    Handheld Communicators

    The Emerson 475 Field Communicator (successor to the 375) is the standard tool for HART communication in the field:

    • Auto-detects connected HART devices
    • Reads all process variables, configuration, and diagnostics
    • Writes configuration (range, damping, engineering units, tag)
    • Stores device configurations for offline comparison
    • Compatible with FOUNDATION Fieldbus devices as well

    3.4 Rosemount 3051 — The Industry Standard Pressure Transmitter

    The Rosemount 3051 family from Emerson is the most widely deployed pressure transmitter in the world. It uses a capacitance-based sensing cell — a Hastelloy C diaphragm deflects under pressure, changing the capacitance of a sensing element. The capacitance change is converted to a 4–20 mA output with ±0.065% reference accuracy.

    3051 Model Variants

    Model Measurement Range
    3051C Differential pressure (ΔP) 0.1 inH₂O to 2000 PSID
    3051T Gauge or absolute pressure 0.3 to 4000 PSIG
    3051L Liquid level (flanged) For direct tank mounting
    3051S Scalable, modular platform Coplanar, traditional, or remote seal

    Remote Seals

    When the process fluid is corrosive, high-temperature, viscous, or prone to plugging impulse lines, the sensing cell connects to the process through a remote seal filled with fill fluid (silicone, glycerin, or Halocarbon). The fill fluid transmits pressure without contacting the sensing cell.

    Common remote seal applications:

    • High-temperature steam (capillary fill fluid rated to 400°F)
    • Slurry service (flush diaphragm — no cavity to plug)
    • Corrosive acids (PTFE-lined wetted parts)
    • Sanitary processes (3A/FDA-compliant flush diaphragm)

    Reading a 3051 Loop

    24 VDC supply (from DCS I/O card)
    │
    ├── (+) wire → Rosemount 3051 (+) terminal
    │
    Rosemount 3051 (−) terminal → 250Ω resistor → (−) return
                                        │
                                  HART modem reads
                                  voltage across resistor
                                  (4mA × 250Ω = 1V at zero,
                                   20mA × 250Ω = 5V at full scale)

    The DCS I/O card reads 1–5 VDC across the 250Ω resistor and scales it to engineering units.

    3.5 Temperature Measurement

    Temperature is the most common process measurement. Two primary sensor technologies dominate:

    RTDs (Resistance Temperature Detectors)

    An RTD uses a platinum wire whose resistance increases predictably with temperature:

    $$R_T = R_0 \left[1 + AT + BT^2 + CT^3\right]$$

    For PT100 (100Ω at 0°C): A = 3.9083×10⁻³, B = −5.775×10⁻⁷, C = −4.183×10⁻¹²

    RTD Type Resistance at 0°C Accuracy Range
    PT100 100 Ω ±0.1°C −200 to +850°C
    PT1000 1000 Ω ±0.1°C −200 to +850°C

    RTDs are preferred when accuracy is paramount. They are slower to respond than thermocouples and require a transmitter to convert resistance to 4–20 mA.

    Thermocouples

    A thermocouple generates a millivolt EMF proportional to the temperature difference between the measuring junction and a reference junction (cold junction). Common types:

    Type Materials Range Application
    J Iron / Constantan −40 to +750°C General purpose
    K Chromel / Alumel −200 to +1260°C Most common industrial
    E Chromel / Constantan −200 to +900°C Highest sensitivity
    T Copper / Constantan −200 to +370°C Cryogenic, food
    S Platinum/Rhodium 0 to +1600°C High-temperature, furnaces

    Thermocouples are rugged, fast-responding, and cover extreme temperatures. They are less accurate than RTDs and require cold junction compensation.

    Rosemount 644 Temperature Transmitter

    The Rosemount 644 is the standard head-mounted or DIN-rail temperature transmitter for both RTD and thermocouple inputs:

    • Universal input: accepts PT100/PT1000 RTDs, all thermocouple types, millivolt
    • HART 7 communication
    • Sensor backup: dual-sensor input with automatic failover
    • Sensor drift alert: Compares two sensors and alerts if they diverge beyond threshold
    • ±0.1°C reference accuracy with PT100 input

    3.6 Flow Measurement Overview

    Flow measurement is the most diverse measurement category — multiple physical principles, each suited to specific applications.

    Differential Pressure (DP) Flow

    The oldest and most widely used flow measurement method. A primary element (orifice plate, venturi, pitot tube) creates a pressure drop proportional to flow squared:

    $$Q = K \cdot A \cdot \sqrt{\frac{2\Delta P}{\rho}}$$

    Where K is the discharge coefficient, A is the restriction area, and ρ is fluid density.

    Orifice plates are the most common primary element: inexpensive, no moving parts, well-characterized. The Rosemount 3051SFC is a purpose-built DP flow transmitter that integrates the DP measurement with static pressure and temperature sensing to perform multivariable flow compensation — correcting the volumetric flow to mass flow in real time without an external flow computer.

    Limitation: DP flow accuracy degrades at low flow (the relationship is √ΔP, so low flow = very low ΔP). Turndown is typically 4:1 to 10:1 without special ranging.

    Vortex Flow — Rosemount 8800

    A vortex flowmeter measures the frequency of vortices shed by a bluff body inserted in the flow stream. Strouhal number relates vortex frequency to velocity:

    $$f = S_t \cdot \frac{V}{d}$$

    The Rosemount 8800D is the industry-leading vortex meter:

    Feature Specification
    Fluids Liquid, gas, steam
    Accuracy ±0.65% of rate (liquid), ±1.0% (gas/steam)
    Turndown 20:1 (liquid), 15:1 (gas)
    Line sizes ½" to 12" wafer or flange
    Output HART 7 or FOUNDATION Fieldbus
    Temperature −200°C to +427°C process temperature

    Best applications: Steam flow (no moving parts, handles wet steam), utilities (air, nitrogen, steam), clean liquid service. Not suitable for slurries, very viscous fluids, or very low flow velocities.

    The 8800D multivariable version includes integral temperature and pressure sensing for compensated mass flow of steam and gas without external transmitters — three measurements in one device.

    Coriolis Flow — Micro Motion

    Coriolis flowmeters measure mass flow directly using the Coriolis effect: a vibrating tube carrying flow experiences a twist proportional to mass flow rate. No density or viscosity correction required.

    Micro Motion ELITE series (Emerson):

    Feature Specification
    Accuracy ±0.05% of rate (mass flow)
    Density accuracy ±0.0005 g/cc
    Turndown 100:1 or better
    Fluids Any liquid or slurry; dense gas
    Outputs Mass flow, volume flow, density, temperature — all simultaneously
    Sizes 1/10" to 6"

    Why Coriolis wins for custody transfer and batch: Mass flow is the gold standard for fiscal measurement and recipe dosing. No flow conditioning required. Direct density measurement enables real-time concentration calculation.

    Limitation: High cost; large pressure drop for small sensors; not suitable for very low-density gases at low pressure.

    3.7 Level Measurement

    Level measurement determines the height of liquid or solid material in a vessel.

    DP Level

    A differential pressure transmitter connected across a vessel measures the hydrostatic head:

    $$\Delta P = \rho \cdot g \cdot h$$

    Rosemount 3051 with remote seals is the most common approach. Wet leg and dry leg configurations handle different process conditions.

    Limitation: Accuracy depends on constant fluid density. If density varies (temperature, composition), the level reading drifts without compensation.

    Guided Wave Radar (GWR) — Rosemount 5300

    GWR transmits a microwave pulse down a probe inserted into the vessel. The pulse reflects off the liquid surface (dielectric discontinuity) and returns. Time-of-flight gives level:

    $$L = \frac{c \cdot t}{2}$$

    Rosemount 5300 features:

    • Works with liquids, slurries, interfaces (oil/water)
    • Immune to vapor, foam, pressure, and temperature changes
    • No moving parts; minimal maintenance
    • Accuracy: ±3 mm
    • Range: up to 20 m
    • Rigid or flexible probe for tall vessels and bypass chambers

    Non-Contact Radar — Rosemount 5408

    The Rosemount 5408 uses FMCW (Frequency Modulated Continuous Wave) radar to measure level without a probe touching the liquid:

    • 80 GHz radar (narrow beam — ideal for obstructed vessels)
    • ±1 mm accuracy
    • Works on any liquid with dielectric constant > 1.4
    • Suitable for corrosive acids, cryogenic liquids, high-pressure vessels
    • No product contact — zero maintenance

    3.8 WirelessHART — Measurement Without Wires

    WirelessHART (IEC 62591) extends the HART protocol to a self-organizing mesh radio network operating at 2.4 GHz (IEEE 802.15.4):

    • Update rate: 1 second to 60 minutes (configurable)
    • Range: 250 m line-of-sight; mesh extends coverage
    • Security: AES-128 encryption, device join authentication
    • Power: Battery (3–10 years), energy harvesting, or loop-powered
    • Gateway: Rosemount 1410S converts wireless to HART/MODBUS/OPC-UA for DCS integration

    When to choose WirelessHART:

    Situation Reason
    Remote or inaccessible locations No cable trenching cost
    Temporary measurements Deploy, measure, remove
    Added measurement points on existing equipment Cable cost exceeds transmitter cost
    Monitoring-only points Control not required; 15–60 second update acceptable
    Hazardous areas with explosion risk from cabling Intrinsically safe wireless alternative

    When NOT to choose WirelessHART:

    • Control loops requiring < 1 second update rates
    • Applications requiring guaranteed latency (safety systems require wired)
    • Dense steel structures that block radio propagation (mesh helps, but verify)

    3.9 Signal Integrity and Loop Commissioning

    Loop Checking Procedure

    Before a measurement loop goes to service:

    1. Verify wiring polarity — 4–20 mA is polarity-sensitive
    2. Apply process zero — confirm transmitter reads 4.00 mA
    3. Apply process full scale — confirm transmitter reads 20.00 mA
    4. Check DCS scaling — confirm DCS displays correct engineering units at both endpoints
    5. Test HART communication — confirm tag, range, and secondary variables visible from DCS
    6. Document as-found configuration — record in asset management system

    Common Loop Problems

    Symptom Likely cause
    Reading stuck at 3.6 mA Broken wire or transmitter power failure
    Reading stuck at 22 mA Over-range or sensing element failure
    Noisy/fluctuating reading Turbulent process, wrong damping setting, ground loop
    Reading drifts over temperature Thermal zero effect — adjust zero trim at process temperature
    HART not communicating No 250Ω resistor in loop; verify with multimeter

    Chapter Summary

    The 4–20 mA current loop is the universal language of process instrumentation — the live zero distinguishes broken wires from process zeros, and HART superimposes digital data on the same pair of wires. The Rosemount 3051 pressure transmitter covers differential, gauge, and absolute pressure across virtually all process ranges. Rosemount 644 handles RTD and thermocouple temperature. Flow measurement technology selection depends on fluid type, required accuracy, and turndown — DP orifice for cost, vortex for steam, Coriolis for mass flow custody transfer. Radar has largely replaced float and DP for level in difficult services. WirelessHART extends measurement reach without cable cost where update rates of 8–60 seconds are acceptable. All of these technologies are integrated through HART communication into DCS historians, AMS Device Manager, and the plant asset management ecosystem.

    Quiz

    Question 1 — Remember

    What does a 3.6 mA signal on a 4–20 mA loop indicate?

    • A) The process variable is slightly below zero
    • B) The transmitter has detected a fault or the wire is broken
    • C) The transmitter is in manual override
    • D) The HART communication is active

    Question 2 — Understand

    A Rosemount 3051 is ranged 0–100 PSIG. The DCS reads 12 mA. What is the process pressure?

    • A) 12 PSIG
    • B) 25 PSIG
    • C) 50 PSIG (12 mA = 50% of 16 mA span = 50% of 100 PSIG)
    • D) 60 PSIG

    Question 3 — Evaluate

    A plant wants to add 15 temperature monitoring points in a remote tank farm, 400 meters from the control room. The measurements are for monitoring only — no control loops. What is the best instrumentation approach and why?

    • A) Run conduit and 4–20 mA wiring — most reliable
    • B) Use FOUNDATION Fieldbus — fewer wires
    • C) Deploy WirelessHART transmitters with a gateway — eliminates cable cost across 400m; monitoring-only points don't require the <1 second update rate that would rule out wireless
    • D) Use thermocouple extension wire direct to DCS — cheapest sensor cost

    Sales Implications

    Sales bridge — Instrumentation to Applied Control value

    1. The 3051 is everywhere — use it as the entry point. Nearly every process plant has hundreds of Rosemount 3051s. Every one of them can be read via HART. Ask: "Are you using AMS Device Manager to monitor these? We can show you what you're missing in 30 minutes from your DCS workstation."
    2. Wireless is a door-opener. A customer with 15 monitoring gaps and no budget for cable trenching can deploy WirelessHART for 30–40% of the wired cost. Applied Control can size, quote, and commission — position it as a complete solution including gateway, configuration, and DCS integration.
    3. Multivariable meters reduce installed cost. A Rosemount 8800D multivariable replaces three separate transmitters (DP, temperature, pressure) for compensated steam mass flow. The TCO argument writes itself.
    4. Coriolis is the accuracy sell. If a customer is using orifice plates for custody transfer or batch dosing, the Micro Motion accuracy upgrade pays back in reduced product give-away and batch rejects. Get the process engineer and finance talking together.
